## 1. The scale of the pre-imperial history (resolves Q-083)

**The question:** how much older than KE 0 is the pre-imperial history (the Veshna faith/temple, the Orenic world, the pre-imperial polities)?

**The four internal-causality anchors** (each an established canon fact that requires deep time):

1. **The frozen sacred script (CUL-02 §2; REL-02 §1).** The Veshna sacred script is an **older** abugida (~60 characters, the sacred diacritics), **conservative** — "it changes slower than the Orenic script — the sacred text's orthography is frozen." A sacred orthography that stays frozen while a secular script of the same world keeps changing is the signature of a scribal institution **centuries older** than the secular tradition it outlasts. The Orenic script is attested from the founding era (the Beshara records, ADM-02 §2); the sacred script must predate it by a deep margin — and its *freezing* must predate the Orenic's emergence (the Charter, KE 0–35, is written in a script **already frozen** — §4/§10).
2. **The Orenic drift (CUL-02 §1/§4/§5).** The Orenic family has already differentiated, before KE 0, into **three registers** (the Samur core, the 2–3 Tarn lowland varieties, the Veth coastal drift) — and the Tarn varieties carry **"a local substrate (the plain's pre-Samur vocabulary)"** (CUL-02 §4). Dialectal differentiation to the stage of distinct-but-related registers requires **centuries of basin residence** for the whole family, and an **earlier** settlement of the Tarn plain than the plateau's consolidation.
3. **The temple's survival claim (REL-01/REL-02; NS-01 §6).** The matha's authority is its **antiquity over every ruling house** — the rite sanctions temporal powers that rise and fall while the rite persists (the deterrence that is never consummated, REL-02 §8). That claim is credible only if the matha's institutional form (the guru's seat, the rite, the rite-records) predates the **oldest** pre-imperial house (the Orenar line, §4) by a multi-generational margin — and if the temple has **survived multiple great failures** (the wind-law cadence, GEO-03 §2) — the temple's deepest legend is survival through the nights.
4. **The polities' depth (DYN-03; FOR-01/02; ADM-02).** The founding inherits **multi-generational structures, not new ones**: the ~40 minor bār chief districts + the major lines (the Dhresham, the Orenesh — DYN-03 §4), the 3 Tarn houses (the DYN-03 §3), the 4 Khor clans (the DYN-03 §6), the Veth coast's pepper culture + the Veth variant faith (FOR-01 §2; REL-02 §6), the Sareth pass trade (GEO-01 §3/§5). Every one of these needs **200–400+ years** of pre-imperial development to exist at founding-era depth.

**The derivation.** Anchors 1+2 fix a **minimum** of ~600–900 years of Orenic basin residence before KE 0 (the differentiation + the script's lead). Anchor 3 requires the matha's institutional form ~300–500 years before the founding and **multiple great-failure survivals** within the attested span. Anchor 4 requires the polities' own depths. The span satisfying all four — with the long stable periods the world actually had (not a constant of events) — is **~1,200 years**. The **Deep-Age boundary ~KE −1200** is fixed as **the temple's oral chronicle's horizon** — "the Night of the Mountain (~KE −945) is the oldest night we can name" is the world's own epistemic limit: the record before it exists only as **conditions** (the §3), not as events. Beyond ~KE −1200 the pre-imperial depth is **unattested by the contemporaneous record** (the wind's cluster phase, the Orenic residence itself, the Khoric's separation — all older). **→ EXTENDED in TIM-06 (2026-08-28):** the *actual* civilizational depth is now established from the **deep record layer** (the old stone, the old fields, the frozen hill-speech, the half-wild groves, the steppe's memory, the technique inheritances — TIM-06 §3): the Oren basin's civilizational history extends from **at least ~KE −5000** (the strata: the pre-named deep, the Veshna/Khel/Orenam strata, the Quiet Gap — TIM-06 §5 — the setting-required floor, ~5,000 years, met). The ~KE −1200 boundary is preserved as the **record's horizon** — the oldest point the temple's *contemporaneous* record reaches — now **causally a survival horizon** (the refugia's re-gathering — TIM-06 §4); the pre-~−1200 negative-KE dating is the later chronicles' **reconstruction** (the world's own deep-time reckoning is the formulae, not year-numbers — TIM-06 §3). This file's "Deep Age" (the unattested-as-events period) is the world's *experienced* deep; the *actual* deep history is TIM-06 (the same period, two epistemic levels).

**What this is not.** It is not a compression of the world's history (the pre-epoch is ~1,200 years of attested depth *plus* the unattested deep — the Kesra Era's 680+ years remain the *most recent slice*, TIM-04 §1), and it is not a stretch (nothing in the established canon requires more than this; the extra depth would be filler, which the project forbids). ## 2. The wind regime: phases and the attested nights

**The refinement (the GEO-03 §2 clarification).** The wind law (GEO-03) is a **foundational world law** that operated across the whole Pre-Kesra Age. Its **great-failure interval is variable**: the GEO-03 §2 table's "~80–120 years" is the **typical interval of the Deep Age's cluster phase** (before ~KE −1200 — the era the table's cadence was fitted to, the world's deeper, more volatile regime). In the **settled phase** (from ~KE −1200 on), the great failures are **rarer, multi-year, and separated by long quiet stretches** — the attested instances below. The **failed-wind cadence (~7–12 years), the weak-wind (~3–5 years), the strong-wind (~5–7 years), and the Khor bad-grass year (~7–10 years)** operate unchanged in both phases (GEO-03 §2/§3; the Khor steppe's climate is independent of the sea-wind, GEO-03 §3).

**Why the regime settled.** The world's own long wind-oscillation shifted from a cluster regime to a settled regime around ~KE −1200 (the world's fact; the mechanism is **unattested** — it is part of the deep unknown, §10). The settling is the **precondition** of everything the Pre-Kesra Age builds: a stable wind regime is what lets the Orenic basin fill, the plain settle early, the coast's groves expand, and the temple's grain-reserve logic harden into an institution. **The quieting of the winds is the Pre-Kesra Age's deep cause** (the condition that makes the temple polity possible) — the world's environmental history, not an event.

## 3. The starting conditions (~KE −1200 and earlier — the Deep Age)

The Pre-Kesra Age **begins with conditions, not events** (the Deep Age is the world's own unattested horizon — known as the settled state of the world at ~KE −1200, inferred from the later record). The conditions:

**The Orenic world (the peoples and their speech).** The Orenic family (CUL-02 §1) is already resident across the Oren basin, differentiated by zone:
- **The core plateau (the bār heartland):** the future Samur are **one bār chiefdom among several** — a horse-poor, agrarian-military people of the upper Oren plateau (east of the Sareth, south of the Khel confluence — GEO-01 §1/§2, DYN-01 §1), positioned at the junction of the Sareth passes + the upper Oren + the bār fiscal base. Their distinction is **position, not size** (DYN-01 §1) — already, at the Deep Age, they are the chiefdoms nearest the Sareth passes (the eastern lip of the Thren Gate; the pass-watch is their old occupation).
- **The Tarn plain (the east):** settled **earlier** than the plateau's consolidation (the CUL-02 §4 "local substrate" — **determined completely:** the plain's substrate vocabulary is **archaic Orenic** — the plain's older settlement retained older forms while the plateau's speech drifted; there is **no** pre-Orenic people of the plain in the canon, and none is needed). The 2–3 Tarn lowland varieties are already distinguishable.
- **The Veth shore (the south coast):** the **coastal drift** speech; the **pepper (Vetham) groves** on the hill-slopes (the GEO-03 §6/ECO-02 §5 wind-fed slopes — the groves are old, tended by the hill communities); a string of **small ports** (the future Kesveth among them — the *settlement* is old; the imperial *city* is KE 140, TIM-03 §2); the maritime vocabulary from the **old coastal trade tongue** (the CUL-01 §2 "coastal trade pidgin" — at this stage an Orenic-based trade language; its later Phreic component is only from KE 500 on, CUL-02 §1).
- **The Sareth highlands (beyond the west wall):** the **Sarethan hill-people** — the "different world" (GEO-01 §3) **determined completely:** they are a **conservative Orenic hill register** (a fourth Orenic variety, frozen by the mountain isolation — the hill speech keeps old forms the way the sacred script does; the "different world" is a cultural/linguistic impression, not a separate family). The hill terraces (the GEO-02 §2 density: 5–30), the **iron ore** (the Sareth foothills — GEO-01 §5), the **timber** (the Sareth forests), the **copper** (the Sareth foothills — MIL-02 §4). ## 4. The Veshna faith's formation (the Age of the Mountain, ~KE −1150 → −800)

**The mountain shrine.** At the **Veshna mountain** — a shrine in the upper Oren plateau's spring zone, at the **Oren's source/headwaters** (the sacred spring) — the old water-and-mountain cult site becomes the center of the region's ritual life. **Why here (the material cause):** the shrine sits at the **source of the river that feeds the plain** — in a wind-dependent world, the water's source is the plain's life's source; the shrine that guards the spring is the natural center of the agrarian heartland's sacred life. (This is the **geographic kernel** of the Founding Myth's "drank the Oren" image — REL-02 §7; the myth's *exaggeration* and its *distortion* (erasing the chiefs' absorption) remain established and untouched.)

**The crystallization (what happens, and why).** Around the shrine, the **guru–śiṣya lineages** (the priestly class's self-reproduction, REL-01 §2) **systematize** the local ritual practice (the folk-cult substrate, §3) into the **dharma grammar**: *(**Deep root — TIM-06 §5 #5, 2026-08-28:** the lineages are the **spring lineages** — the deep's surviving thread [the mountain's refugium is the deep's only continuous site — TIM-06 §4/§5 #1]; the crystallization is the **re-gathering** of the deep's scattered practice around the surviving spring lineages [TIM-06 §4]; the matha's institutional antiquity — the world's oldest continuous institution — is the causative source of its authority alongside the rite's form [TIM-06 §5 #5].)* the **dharma by station** (king's/priest's/warrior's/merchant's/peasant's dharma — REL-02 §2), the **karma**, the **moksha**, the **varna framework**. **The form is a scholarly crystallization, not a revelation** — there is **no prophet and no single scripture** (REL-01 §1) because the grammar is **compiled by the lineages** from the standing practice over generations; its "purity" (the Charter's "dharma grammar in its purest form," REL-02 §1) is the crystallization's product. **The consequence:** the Veshna faith now has a **grammar** (dispersed authority across the commentarial tradition, the guru–śiṣya lineages, the temple, the local — REL-01 §1) centered on **one sacred site** — the centering is **functional**, not aesthetic: in the wind's world, **the grain's memory is the dharma's memory** — the institution that keeps the grain through the nights (the reserve, §3) is the institution the dharma centers on (the sanctioning power = the power that guarantees survival — §5/§9 #5).

**The proto-consecration (the rite's form — fixed here).** The hereditary rulers of the growing shrine settlement seek the mountain's sanction: a **ritual investiture + a public oath before the shrine**, performed by the head of the priestly council (the **guru's seat** forms). **The load-bearing detail:** the **oath is to a separate temporal power** — the investiture *binds a ruler who is not the priest*. This is the rite's **original form**, and it is **why the matha "sanctions, but does not rule" (NS-01 §6) is not an imperial accident but the rite's design** (the NS-01 §6 structure is **pre-imperial** — §9 #5). The consecration's **publicness** (the court + the city as witnesses — REL-02 §3 "the legitimation is performative") is fixed here too: the rite requires a **space outside the court's direct reach** — the causal root of the later **two-city layout** (Kesra the political center ≠ Veshna the sacred center — GEO-02 §2's "religious counterweight to the court" is a **founding design choice** motivated by the rite's form — §7).

**The first rite-records.** The ritual sounds are **carved on the mountain stone** (the sacred diacritics' first use — CUL-02 §2). These are **rite-records, not literature** — the distinction is the canon's: the Kesra Charter (KE 0–35) remains **the oldest text** (the oldest *literary* work, REL-02 §1) because nothing before it is a *text* — the pre-imperial sacred record is **stone + the oral chronicle** (§10).

**The Orenar line.** The shrine settlement's hereditary rulers — **the Orenar line** (the "of-the-Oren" — the river people; the DYN-02 §1 name pool) — become the **first consecrated line** (the proto-consecration, above). The Orenar are a **temporal power, separate from the matha** (the priestly council + the guru's seat are the **sanctioning** institution, not the rulers — the triad's prototype: temporal ruler + priestly council + the rite that binds them). The temple city grows from the shrine settlement into a small city (~5–10k at its peak — the High-Empire 100–150k, DEM-02 §2, is the imperial growth; the pre-imperial Veshna is small).

**The consequences (constrain the next period):** the faith now has a grammar + a center + a rite + a first consecrated line + stone records; the shrine city is the region's ritual center; the bār chiefdoms begin to seek the mountain's arbitration in their disputes (the rite as arbiter — the temple law's root, §5).

**The layer tags:** what happened — the crystallization, the proto-consecration, the Orenar's rise; what was recorded — the stone inscriptions (the rite-records) + the chronicle's "the mountain's time"; what was believed — the shrine's primacy as the founding of the region's order (later hagiography will make it sacred rather than functional); what was forgotten — the lineages' names, the Orenar individuals; what remains unknown — the name Veshna's origin, the exact crystallization's steps.

## 5. The Temple Period (~KE −800 → −265)

**The temple polity (the bār chiefdoms' league).** The bār chiefdoms (the ~40 minor chiefdoms + the major lines — the **Dhresham line** at the future Besra market site, the **Orenesh line** at the future canal site — the DYN-03 §4 key bār chiefs, **pre-imperial** major chiefdoms) recognize the **Orenar's primacy**: a **loose league** — the chiefdoms send **tribute grain to the temple reserve**; in return they receive **the rite** (consecration of their districts' heads at the mountain for the major lines; arbitration for all) **and the grain in famine**. **The Orenar rule the city; the chiefdoms rule their districts** — the **hereditary-in-practice** pattern (the DYN-01 §2 "the chiefs keep their districts" structure is **pre-imperial**; the founding *formalizes* it, the Chiefs' Compact KE 50, TIM-03 §2). **The succession practice:** the Orenar line's succession is **qualified elective-hereditary** — the rite's sanction + the chiefdoms' consent (the league's oldest practice; the founding's succession system, DYN-01 §4, **inherits** this practice — the "house + the sanctioner + the assembled chiefs" prototype of the later three-way contest).

**The devanama (the temple land's origin).** The temple land begins as the **grain-reserve** (the §3 habit) and becomes **endowed land**: after each night, the chiefdoms donate land to the temple (the reserve that saved them) — the **devanama** (the REL-02 §4 tax-exempt parallel fiscal base) is **born in the Temple Period**, and the **temple evasion** (ADM-01 §7) is **pre-imperial** (the endowments are outside the chiefdoms' tribute from the start). Pre-imperial extent: the Veshna district's arable ~3–5% (growing; the later 10–15% of the arable, REL-02 §4, is the **imperial** expansion — the Great Temple's endowments, KE 240–265).

**The three standing laws (the legal pluralism's origin).** Three legal orders are **standing** before KE 0:
1. **The chiefdom law** — the district custom of the bār/Tarn chiefdoms (the chiefs' word + the panchayat's consent).
2. **The jati law** — the panchayat (the occupational corporations' internal law — the marriage, the guild discipline, the minor disputes; the varna/jāti framework of the dharma grammar, REL-02 §2 — the standing corporate order).

## 6. The Long Night (KE −265 → −250)

**The event (what happened, and its mechanism).** A **multi-year great failure** — a **run of failed winds** (three failed-wind years within ~10 years; the catastrophic core ~KE −262 → −255 — the GEO-03 §2 failed-wind cadence clustering at the settled regime's lowest point) — the **hardest night of the settled phase** and the **last before the founding**. The mechanism (the GEO-03 §4 causal chain, at its extreme): the pell fails **three years running** → the Oren runs low → the delta/Tarn/Veth famine → the debt-less pre-epoch form (the §9 #6 distinction: pre-imperial, there is **no silver tax and no moneylender class** — the chain runs **famine → flight → fragmentation**, not **famine → debt → revolt**).

**The consequences by zone (each consequence alters the later conditions):**
- **The Tarn plain:** devastated (the pell's three-year failure — the contraction ~30–50% — §11). The houses **fragment**: the inter-house rivalry over the crossing tolls (the last league cohesion dissolves — the tolls become each house's private income, the ADM-02 §3 evasion mechanism's **root**: the houses already under-report and keep the surplus, because the **central collector no longer exists** — the KE 490 "Tarn tribute crisis" (TIM-03 §5) is the **imperial** re-imposition of a collection the pre-epoch lost). - **The Veth coast:** broken (the pepper fails — the GEO-03 §6 "a failed wind → the pepper fails → the price spikes" — at the catastrophic extreme: the groves are **abandoned** (the labor-intensive cure, ECO-02 §5, cannot be worked in famine); the ports **collapse into a patchwork** (the hill chiefs withdraw from the coast; the coastal drift culture halves). **The consequence:** the coast is a **patchwork at KE 0** — Vethan I's founding (~KE 150, FOR-02 §2) is a **re-consolidation of a broken coast**, not a founding ex nihilo (the FOR-01 §2 "consolidation of the Veth coast" is now **caused**).
- **The delta:** **emptied** (the water-crossed wetland, without the pell, is unlivable — the Orenam fringe retreats to the river; the delta's settlements are gone). **The consequence:** the delta is **no-city** at KE 0 — **Voren (KE 155, TIM-03 §2) is a new port in an emptied delta** (the first mouth-port ever — §9 #3).
