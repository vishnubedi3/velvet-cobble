## 1. The regions (the provinces)

The territory divides into **seven regions**, each defined by geography and tied to the center by a specific relationship:

1. **The Samur** (the heartland, the upper Oren plateau) — the founding region; the political/legitimacy core. East of the Sareth, south of the Khel confluence.
2. **The Middle Oren** (the bār fiscal core) — the grain belt; the fiscal heartland; the largest and most populous region.
3. **The Sareth Marches** (the west wall) — the passes, the garrisons, the iron/timber routes; a narrow, militarized border region.
4. **The Khor Frontier** (the north) — the steppe edge; the horse trade, the salt/silver; a garrison + emporium region.
5. **The Tarn Marches** (the east periphery) — the tributary paddy plain; the future fragmentation site.
6. **The Oren Delta** (the south port zone) — the maritime trade hub; the delta-mouth chokepoint.
7. **The Veth Shore** (the south coast) — the pepper coast; the empire's coastal interest opposite Vethra.

**Relationship to the center — three control mechanisms, matched to the geography:**
- The **Middle Oren + the Samur** (the bār core) are **directly administered** — the fiscal heartland; the tax grain moves down the Oren.
- The **Sareth Marches + the Khor Frontier** (the hard borders) are **garrisoned** — the passes, the standing presence, the horse trade.
- The **Tarn Marches** (the remote paddy east) are **held by tribute** — nominal control, strong local elites (the fragmentation site).
- The **Oren Delta + the Veth Shore** (the coast) are **traded / port-controlled** — the delta mouth, the pepper; increasingly shared with the Phre (Phase 5).

This **direct / garrison / tribute (+ port) control** is the geographic expression of the **composite monarchy** — the empire rules its regions in different ways *because the geography demands it*.

## 2. The cities (each justified)

| City | Region | Why it exists (geographic / economic / strategic) | Relationship to the center |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kesra** — the imperial capital | the Samur | the political/legitimacy center: at the heart of the bār fiscal base; on the Oren (logistics); defensible (the Sareth behind, the river to the east); the Samur founding heartland | the center itself; the seat of the dynasty |
| **Veshna** — the temple city | the Samur | the founding faith's center; a **temple-economy anchor** (land grants, the priestly bureaucracy, the craft workshops, the fairs) — legitimation + fiscal sink | a short distance from Kesra; the religious counterweight to the court |
| **Besra** — the grain market | the Middle Oren | the main **bār grain-gathering and river-shipping point**; the largest market town; the **fiscal collection point** (the tax grain is assembled and shipped down the Oren) | the fiscal hub; the city that feeds the court and the army |
| **Threna** — the western fortress | the Sareth Marches | the fortified town at the **main Sareth pass** (the Thren Gate); the **western defense** (the chokepoint) + the **main west trade town** (iron/timber in, bār out) | the western garrison; the empire's first line against the west |
| **Gheshar** — the waystation | the Sareth Marches | a smaller town at the **second pass** (the Vesh Gate); the **timber/iron route** | a secondary garrison / trade node |
| **Keshkhor** — the horse emporium | the Khor Frontier | the **northern frontier garrison + the great horse market** (the trade with the Khor; salt/silver); the **southern edge of the steppe** | the northern garrison + the horse-trade hub; the empire's Khor dependency made concrete |
| **Voren** — the delta port | the Oren Delta | the great **port at the Oren mouth**; the **maritime trade hub** (the pepper, the textiles, the sea-lanes); the **delta-mouth chokepoint** (whoever holds it controls the Oren trade + the reverse-invasion axis) | the economic/maritime hub; its capture would be the empire's greatest strategic loss |
| **Kesveth** — the Veth port | the Veth Shore | the empire's **coastal town on the Veth shore** (the pepper trade, the coast opposite Vethra) | the southern coastal interest; the frontier of the Vethra rivalry |

**The key structural tension:** the **political capital (Kesra, in the heartland)** and the **economic/maritime hub (Voren, in the delta)** are **different cities, separated by the length of the Oren.** The political/legitimacy center sits in the fiscal heartland, but the economic/maritime center sits at the coast. As the coast grows richer (the Phre, the pepper), the **economic center of gravity shifts toward the delta**, pulling against the political center at Kesra. (A seed for Phase 4/5 — the ADM/ECO/FOR tension and the Phre's leverage.)

## 3. The passes (resolves Q-001)

The Sareth has **three main passes + minor mule tracks**, one dominant:

1. **The Thren Gate** — the **main** pass; the **primary invasion/trade route**; wide enough for wheeled traffic and cavalry; the fortified town of **Threna** at its inner (east) lip.
2. **The Vesh Gate** — the **second** pass; narrower; the **timber/iron route** (mules, ox-carts); the waystation **Gheshar**.
3. **The Ghul Gate** — the **third** pass; a **high, seasonal military route** (snow-bound part of the year); no major town — a garrison post only.

**Strategic logic:** an invader from the west is **funneled to the Thren Gate** (where the empire is waiting at Threna), while the empire's **own projection** (iron/timber) uses the **Vesh Gate**. The two directions of traffic are **separated by pass** — the defense and the trade do not use the same route.

## 4. The borders (each justified)

- **The western border (the Sareth)** — a mountain wall; a **defensible, militarized border** (the passes, the garrisons). *Justification:* the iron/timber source is beyond it, so the empire must project west, but the wall makes defense cheap.
- **The northern border (the Khor)** — the **open steppe; no wall**; a **garrison + mobile cavalry + the horse-trade emporium** at Keshkhor. *Justification:* the horses come from here, so the border is a **trade frontier as much as a military one**.
- **The eastern border (the Tarn Marches)** — a **soft, tributary border** (the paddy plain, the local elites). *Justification:* the plain is rich but remote and water-crossed, so the empire holds it by tribute, not garrison.
- **The southern border (the Veth Shore + the sea)** — a **contested coastal border** (opposite Vethra) + a **maritime border** (the delta mouth, the sea-lanes, the Phre). *Justification:* the pepper/coastal wealth is here, and the maritime power arrives here.

**Strategic summary:** the empire's borders are **four different kinds** (a wall in the west, an open steppe in the north, a tribute plain in the east, a contested coast in the south) — so it needs **four different border strategies** (garrison the passes, hold the steppe by presence + trade, extract tribute from the plain, contest the coast). No single border strategy works for all four. This is the geographic root of the **fiscal-military overstretch** (Phase 5).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the **political center (Kesra)**, **fiscal hub (Besra)**, **maritime hub (Voren)**, **western defense (Threna)**, **northern horse trade (Keshkhor)**, and **southern pepper interest (Kesveth)** are fixed by geography.
- **Unintended:** the **political-vs-economic center tension** (Kesra vs. Voren) grows as the coast richens; the **direct/garrison/tribute control** makes the empire **uneven** (strong in the core, weak in the periphery) — the site of the later fragmentation.
- **Long-term:** **Voren (the delta)** becomes the **Phre's power-base** (the reverse-invasion axis up the Oren); the **Tarn Marches** become the **successor-state incubator**; the **Kesra–Voren axis** becomes the **empire's strategic spine** (and its single greatest vulnerability).
