## 1. The calendar (the Kesra Era)

- **The Kesra Era (KE):** **Year 0 = the accession of Kesra I** (the founding / the unification of the bār core — DYN-01 §1). All imperial dating is in **KE years** (e.g., "KE 120" = 120 years after the founding).
- **The calendar is lunisolar** (the Sanatan logic): **12 lunar months + an occasional intercalary month** (the *adhimāsa*) to keep the lunar year aligned with the wind/solar year.
- **The year begins with the first sea-wind** (the start of the wet season). The calendar is **anchored to the seasonal wind** (GEO-01 §7) — the harvest (the bār and the pell cycles, DEM-01), the fiscal year (the tax at the harvest), and the campaign season (the dry season) are all derived from the wind. **A failed wind is therefore a calendar/event shock** (the famine → debt → revolt chain, DEM-01 §4).
- The month names, the festival year, and the era's civil/religious reckoning are fixed in **CUL-01**.

## 2. The five eras

| Era | KE dates | Defining character |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The Founding** | **0 – 60** | Kesra I unifies the bār core; the chiefs are absorbed; the Veshna temple is co-opted; the **dual structure** (the King + the Shreshtha, the temple as sanctioner) and the succession system are established. |
| **2. The Expansion** | **60 – 220** | The bār core is consolidated; the empire **projects to the four borders** (the Sareth west, the Khor north, the Tarn east, the Veth south); the frontier towns and ports are founded (Threna, Keshkhor, the Tarn tributary, Kesveth, **Voren**); the **first containment of Vethra** begins. |
| **3. The High Empire** | **220 – 380** | The **three-front empire at its peak** (the Sareth + Khor + coast held simultaneously); the **fiscal peak** (the monetization chain strongest); the **population peak** (the good-wind decades, DEM-01); the **great building era** (the Veshna great temple, the expansion of Kesra, the Oren irrigation/roads); the **merchant/bankers at their zenith** (the growth imperative). **Late High Empire (340–380): the first overstretch** — the three-front condition begins to strain the fiscal base; the first failed-wind famine; the chiefs begin to accumulate power. |
| **4. The Stress** (the long process) | **380 – 500** | A **long process of interacting pressures** (Phase 5): **fiscal stress** (the three-front overstretch; the revenue targets inflate; the monetization chain breaks — the peasant → moneylender → revolt) + **elite competition** (the King vs. the Shreshtha; the chiefs' regionalization; the temple as kingmaker) + **environmental pressure** (the repeated failed winds; a **catastrophic wind-failure ~KE 450–480** — the great famine; a **major epidemic ~KE 460–490** — the "plague"; the population contracts, the labor supply breaks, DEM-01). |
| **5. The Fragmentation** (the triggers + the collapse) | **500 – 600+** | The **triggers** act on the root causes: the **Great Succession Crisis (~KE 520–560)** — the contested succession (the "eldest vs. the most worthy" + the three-way contest) divides the empire among the claimants (the Mughal 1707 pattern); the **Phre arrives via the delta (~KE 500–560)** — the chartered company makes the delta mouth its power-base (the reverse-invasion axis); **Vethra rises** (the "state too strong for its size" becomes the dominant southern power). The **composite monarchy collapses** into the **successor states** (the chiefs' districts → states); the **House of Kesra ends (~KE 650–700)**; the **Veshna matha survives** as a religious institution (the mobile node). |

## 3. The anchor events (the dated events that anchor the eras)

*(These are the era-anchors; the full dated event list is TIM-02+. Dates are era-typical, to be fixed as the specific events are written.)*

- **KE 0** — the **founding**: Kesra I's accession; the unification of the bār core; the **consecration** (the temple sanctions the first king); the founding of the capital **Kesra**. (The **Veshna temple pre-dates the empire** — the dynasty co-opts a pre-existing sacred center, DYN-01 §2.)
- **KE ~30–60** — the **dual structure is institutionalized** (the Shreshtha office, the succession system); the first succession (Kesra I → Kesra II) runs **without** a crisis (the system works, for now).
- **KE ~80–120** — **Threna** garrisoned (the west wall); **Keshkhor** founded (the northern horse emporium).
- **KE ~120–180** — the **Tarn tributary** established (the east); **Kesveth** founded (the Veth port).
- **KE ~150–200** — **Voren** founded (the delta port / the maritime hub).
- **KE ~200–240** — the **first war with Vethra** (the first containment — the "state too strong for its size" rivalry begins, GEO-01 §8.5).
- **KE ~220–380** — the **High Empire**: the fiscal/population peak; the **Veshna great temple** built (the great building era); the **first overstretch** in the late High Empire (KE ~340–380).
- **KE ~380–500** — the **Stress**: the repeated failed winds; the **first major peasant revolt** (the monetization chain breaks, ~KE 400–430); the **great famine** (the catastrophic wind-failure, ~KE 450–480); the **great epidemic** (the "plague," ~KE 460–490).
- **KE ~500–560** — the **Phre arrives** (the chartered company, via the delta); the **Great Succession Crisis** (~KE 520–560).
- **KE ~600–700** — the **Fragmentation**: the successor states (the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the Khor north); the **Phre's paramountcy** (the delta mouth); **Vethra's rise**; the **end of the House of Kesra** (~KE 650–700).

## 4. The imperial lifecycle — root causes vs. trigger events

**Root causes (long-term, structural — seeded by the founding and the geography):**
1. **The three-front overstretch** (GEO-01 §8.1) — the empire must hold the Sareth + the Khor + the coast simultaneously; the cost of holding eventually exceeds the revenue of holding.
2. **The fiscal-military tension** (DEM-01 §2) — a small, expensive garrisoned border defending a large, dense, taxable core; the **labor supply is cyclical** (the wind + the epidemic).
3. **The monetization chain** (GEO-01 §8.4) — frontier silver → core cash → cash crops → moneylender → peasant debt → **revolt when the targets inflate**.
4. **The dual-elite fragilization** (DYN-01 §5) — the **founding elite** (the Samur) fragilizes (the Qing banner decay) while the **absorbed elite** (the chiefs) accumulates power (the regionalization).
5. **The succession fault line** (DYN-01 §4) — the "eldest vs. the most worthy" + the **three-way contest** (the house, the Shreshtha, the temple) makes every succession a potential crisis.
6. **The merchant/banker growth imperative** (DYN-01 §5) — the debt-financed war must keep conquering; the empire is structurally pulled toward expansion.

**Trigger events (specific, contingent — they act *on* the root causes, they do not cause them):**
1. **The Great Succession Crisis (~KE 520–560)** — the contested succession divides the empire among the claimants (the Mughal 1707 pattern). *The trigger that converts the elite competition into a fragmentation.*
2. **The catastrophic wind-failure (~KE 450–480)** — the great famine breaks the labor supply (the DEM-01 cycle at its extreme). *The trigger that breaks the fiscal base.*
3. **The great epidemic (~KE 460–490)** — the "plague" empties the fields and breaks the dynasty's legitimacy. *The trigger that compounds the famine.*
4. **The Phre's arrival (~KE 500–560)** — the chartered company makes the delta mouth its power-base. *The trigger that captures the economic center of gravity (Voren) and adds a reverse-invasion axis.*
5. **Vethra's rise (~KE 550–650)** — the "state too strong" becomes the dominant southern power. *The trigger that removes the empire's southern balance.*

**The interaction (why the decline is a *process*, not an event):** the **root causes** (the overstretch, the fiscal-military tension, the monetization chain, the dual-elite fragilization, the succession fault line, the growth imperative) build up **over the whole High Empire and the Stress** (KE 220–500). The **triggers** (the succession crisis, the famine, the epidemic, the Phre, Vethra) then **act on those built-up stresses** in the Fragmentation (KE 500–600+). **None of the triggers alone could have fragmented the empire** — a strong, unified empire would have absorbed any single trigger. It is the **interaction of the long root causes with the late triggers** that produces the fragmentation. (This is the Phase 5 "imperial lifecycle" logic: **root causes vs. trigger events**.)

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the **Kesra Era** is the empire's dating system (Year 0 = the founding); the **five eras** (the Founding, the Expansion, the High Empire, the Stress, the Fragmentation) are the periodization; the **anchor events** are dated (era-typically); the **root-cause/trigger distinction** is established.
- **Unintended:** the **calendar anchored to the wind** means a **failed wind is a calendar/event shock** (the famine → debt → revolt chain is built into the timekeeping); the **long Stress era** (KE 380–500) means the decline is a **~120-year process**, not a single event (the empire is "declining" for a century before it "fragments").
- **Long-term:** the **periodization** is the skeleton that every later file (the kings, the wars, the famines, the Phre) hangs on; the **root-cause/trigger distinction** is the analytic tool for the Phase 5 causality tracking; the **end of the House of Kesra (~KE 650–700)** is the horizon of the "Samur Empire" (the successor states are a *post*-imperial phase).
