> TIM-02 is the **dated event timeline** with **causality tracking** (the Phase 5 requirement). For each major reform, war, and crisis: the **causes** (root + immediate), the **actors** (the five factions + the foreign powers), the **immediate effects**, the **winners/losers**, and the **second-order consequences** (how the decision constrains the next generation). The **specific** details (the individual kings, the commanders, the exact dates) are TIM-03+ and the later DYN/FOR files. Dates are era-typical (KE).

**The causality-tracing rule:** every event's **second-order consequences** become the **causes** of the next event. A decision made in one generation **constrains** the next.

## Era 1 — The Founding (0–60)

### KE 0 — The Founding (Kesra I's accession)

- **Causes:** (root) the Samur's position at the Sareth/Oren/bār junction (GEO-01); (immediate) Kesra I's control of the chokepoint (the Sareth passes + the upper Oren) + the fiscal base (the bār core).
- **Actors:** the Samur (Kesra I, the founding warrior band), the absorbed chiefs (the pre-existing agrarian-military aristocracy), the Veshna matha (the pre-existing temple).
- **Immediate effects:** the unification of the bār core; the chiefs absorbed into the revenue-military system (the hereditary-in-practice districts); the Veshna co-opted (the consecration, the king as protector of dharma); the **dual structure** (the King + the Shreshtha, the temple as sanctioner) established.
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the Samur (Kesra I, the founding elite), the Veshna matha (the sanctioner, the kingmaker). Losers — the absorbed chiefs (their autonomy is curtailed, but they keep their districts).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **dual structure** (the King vs. the Shreshtha fault line) is institutionalized; the **chiefs' hereditary districts** (the regional power base, the successor-state incubator); the **succession system** (the three-way contest — the house, the Shreshtha, the temple); the **temple as kingmaker** (the sanction, the de-legitimation).

### KE ~30–60 — The First Succession (Kesra I → Kesra II)

- **Causes:** (root) the succession system (the three-way contest); (immediate) Kesra I's death.
- **Actors:** the royal house (the agnates), the Shreshtha (the preference), the Veshna matha (the sanction).
- **Immediate effects:** the succession **without a crisis** (the system works, for now) — the "most worthy" agnate is chosen, the Shreshtha and the temple concur, the consecration is performed.
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the chosen king (Kesra II), the temple (the sanction is honored), the Shreshtha (the preference is honored). Losers — the rival agnates (the "eldest" who was passed over, the "most worthy" who lost).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **succession system is proven to work** (the three-way contest can be managed) — but the **rival agnates** (the passed-over) are a **latent fault line** (they will resurface in a later, weaker succession — the Great Succession Crisis).

## Era 2 — The Expansion (60–220)

### KE ~80–180 — The Frontiers (the Sareth west, the Khor north, the Tarn east, the Veth south)

- **Causes:** (root) the growth imperative (the debt-financed war must keep conquering, ECO-01 §5) + the resource scarcity (the horses, the iron/timber, the pepper, GEO-01 §5); (immediate) the bār core is consolidated, the empire projects to the four borders.
- **Actors:** the Shreshtha (the expansion, the campaigns), the military houses (the frontier generals), the chiefs (the Tarn tributary), the merchant/bankers (the shahukar, the war finance), the Khor (the horse trade), the Vethra (the southern rivalry).
- **Immediate effects:** the four frontiers established — the **Sareth** (Threna garrisoned, the western defense), the **Khor** (Keshkhor, the horse emporium, the northern trade), the **Tarn** (the tributary, the eastern extraction), the **Veth** (Kesveth, the pepper coast). **Voren** founded (the delta port, ~KE 150–200).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the Shreshtha (the power, the expansion), the military houses (the frontier generals, the commands), the merchant/bankers (the war finance, the trade), the Khor (the horse trade, the silver/salt). Losers — the Tarn chiefs (the tribute, the extraction), the Vethra (the containment, the rivalry).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **three-front condition** (the Sareth + the Khor + the coast held simultaneously — the standing overstretch); the **Khor horse dependency** (the strategic vulnerability — a failed Khor = no cavalry); the **Tarn tributary** (the regional power base, the fragmentation site); the **Voren** (the maritime hub, the economic center of gravity — the Phre's later target); the **Vethra rivalry** (the "state too big," the containment).

### KE ~200–240 — The First Vethra War (the First Containment)

- **Causes:** (root) the Vethra's rise (the "state too big," FOR-01 §2) + the Samur's growth imperative (the expansion); (immediate) the Vethra's consolidation of the Veth coast (the pepper, the irrigation, the craft-guilds) exceeds what the Samur will tolerate.
- **Actors:** the Shreshtha (the command), the military houses (the field army), the Vethra (the Veth court, the craft-guilds), the merchant/bankers (the war finance).
- **Immediate effects:** the Samur **contains** the Vethra (the first containment) — the Vethra is checked but **not destroyed** (the "state too big" survives); the **Vethra wars** become a **recurring** conflict (the High Empire + the Stress).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the Shreshtha (the command, the prestige), the military houses (the campaigns). Losers — the Vethra (the containment, the checked expansion), the merchant/bankers (the war finance, the debt).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **Vethra rivalry** becomes a **standing** conflict (the recurring Vethra wars — the southern drain); the **Vethra is not destroyed** (the "state too big" survives — it will rise in the Fragmentation); the **war finance** (the debt-financed war — the growth imperative is reinforced).

## Era 3 — The High Empire (220–380)

### KE ~220–380 — The Great Building Era (the Veshna great temple, the Oren irrigation/roads)

- **Causes:** (root) the fiscal/population peak (the good-wind decades, DEM-01) + the court as the market anchor (the state demand, ECO-01 §6); (immediate) the surplus (the bār core) funds the great works.
- **Actors:** the King (the patronage, the legitimation), the Veshna matha (the great temple, the consecration), the Shreshtha (the direction, the administration), the merchant/bankers (the court demand, the trade), the peasants (the corvée, the labor).
- **Immediate effects:** the **Veshna great temple** built (the sacred center, the fiscal sink, the parallel landholding); the **Oren irrigation/roads** (the surplus base, the logistics spine); the **Kesra** expanded (the court, the market anchor); the **fiscal peak** (the revenue, the silver).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the Veshna matha (the great temple, the sanction, the parallel landholding), the King (the patronage, the legitimation), the Shreshtha (the administration, the power), the merchant/bankers (the court demand, the trade). Losers — the peasants (the corvée, the forced labor, the resentment).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **Veshna great temple** (the sacred center — the matha's power is cemented, the parallel landholding grows); the **Oren irrigation/roads** (the surplus base — but the corvée resentment is a latent fault line); the **fiscal peak** (the high water mark — the later decline is measured from here); the **"golden age" myth** (the historical memory, CUL-01 §5).

### KE ~340–380 — The First Overstretch (the turning point)

- **Causes:** (root) the three-front condition (the Sareth + the Khor + the coast, GEO-01 §8.1) + the growth imperative (the debt-financed war, ECO-01 §5) + the Khor horse dependency (the strategic vulnerability); (immediate) the cost of holding the three fronts exceeds the revenue of holding; the **first failed-wind famine** (the pell, the volatile base, DEM-01).
- **Actors:** the Shreshtha (the overstretch, the campaigns), the military houses (the frontier generals, the garrisons), the chiefs (the accumulation of power, the regionalization), the merchant/bankers (the war finance, the debt), the peasants (the famine, the debt, the latent revolt).
- **Immediate effects:** the **three-front condition** begins to **strain the fiscal base** (the cost of holding > the revenue of holding); the **first failed-wind famine** (the pell, the debt, the resentment); the **chiefs begin to accumulate power** (the hereditary districts, the regionalization — the successor-state incubator).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the chiefs (the accumulation of power, the regionalization). Losers — the Shreshtha (the overstretch, the fiscal strain), the peasants (the famine, the debt), the merchant/bankers (the debt, the war finance).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **overstretch** (the standing fiscal strain — the root cause of the Stress); the **failed-wind famine** (the debt, the resentment — the latent revolt); the **chiefs' accumulation of power** (the regionalization — the successor-state incubator); the **turning point** (the High Empire's peak is passed — the decline begins).

## Era 4 — The Stress (380–500)

### KE ~400–430 — The First Major Peasant Revolt (the monetization chain breaks)

- **Causes:** (root) the monetization chain (the silver → the cash → the cash crop → the moneylender → the debt, ECO-01 §2) + the overstretch (the fiscal strain, the revenue targets inflate, TIM-01) + the failed-wind famine (the debt, the resentment, KE ~340–380); (immediate) the revenue targets inflate, the peasants cannot pay, the moneylender debt is unbearable.
- **Actors:** the peasants (the bār core, the pell coast — the majority), the moneylenders (the shahukar, the debt), the Shreshtha (the revenue targets, the monetization), the chiefs (the evasion, the surplus), the Veshna matha (the parallel economy, the temple lands).
- **Immediate effects:** the **first major peasant revolt** (the monetization chain breaks) — the peasants revolt against the inflated targets, the moneylender debt, the cash-crop coercion; the **revenue is disrupted** (the fiscal base is strained).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the peasants (the revolt, the relief from the targets — temporarily). Losers — the Shreshtha (the revenue, the fiscal strain), the moneylenders (the debt, the default), the merchant/bankers (the monetization, the disruption).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **monetization chain is broken** (the social fault line — the revolt is a standing threat); the **revenue is disrupted** (the fiscal strain — the root cause of the Stress); the **peasant resentment** (the latent revolt — it will recur); the **Shreshtha's weakness** (the revenue, the control — the Shreshtha's dominance is undermined).

### KE ~450–480 — The Great Famine (the catastrophic wind-failure)

- **Causes:** (root) the wind dependency (the pell, the volatile base, DEM-01) + the failed-wind cycle (the ~5–15 year range, DEM-01 §4); (immediate) the **catastrophic wind-failure** (the great famine — the pell fails, the debt, the starvation).
- **Actors:** the peasants (the bār core, the pell coast — the famine, the starvation), the moneylenders (the debt, the default), the Shreshtha (the revenue, the fiscal strain), the chiefs (the evasion, the surplus), the Veshna matha (the parallel economy, the temple lands).
- **Immediate effects:** the **great famine** (the catastrophic wind-failure) — the pell fails, the debt is unbearable, the starvation; the **population contracts** (the labor supply breaks, DEM-01 §4); the **revenue collapses** (the fiscal base is broken).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the chiefs (the evasion, the surplus — they keep their land). Losers — the peasants (the famine, the starvation, the debt), the Shreshtha (the revenue, the fiscal collapse), the moneylenders (the debt, the default), the Veshna matha (the parallel economy — the temple lands are strained).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **population contract** (the labor supply breaks — the fiscal-military system is broken, DEM-01 §5); the **revenue collapse** (the fiscal base is broken — the root cause of the Stress); the **dynasty's legitimacy is strained** (the king cannot protect the people — the "protector of dharma" is questioned); the **great famine** (the historical memory, CUL-01 §5 — the "decline" narrative).

### KE ~460–490 — The Great Epidemic (the "plague")

- **Causes:** (root) the dense core + the Oren highway + the Voren port (the epidemic vector, DEM-01 §4) + the great famine (the weakened population, the starvation); (immediate) the **major epidemic** (the "plague" — the disease travels the river and the sea).
- **Actors:** the peasants (the bār core, the pell coast — the epidemic, the death), the Oren (the highway, the vector), the Voren (the port, the vector), the Shreshtha (the revenue, the fiscal strain), the Veshna matha (the parallel economy, the temple lands).
- **Immediate effects:** the **great epidemic** (the "plague") — the disease travels the river and the sea, the population contracts further, the labor supply breaks; the **dynasty's legitimacy is broken** (the king cannot protect the people — the "protector of dharma" is questioned).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — none (the epidemic is a universal loss). Losers — the peasants (the death, the starvation), the Shreshtha (the revenue, the fiscal collapse), the Veshna matha (the parallel economy — the temple lands are strained), the Vethra (the southern rivalry — the epidemic is a mutual loss).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **population contract** (the labor supply breaks — the fiscal-military system is broken, DEM-01 §5); the **dynasty's legitimacy is broken** (the king cannot protect the people — the "protector of dharma" is questioned); the **great epidemic** (the historical memory, CUL-01 §5 — the "decline" narrative); the **Shreshtha's weakness** (the revenue, the control — the Shreshtha's dominance is undermined).

### KE ~480–500 — The Shreshtha's Dominance (the weak king)

- **Causes:** (root) the dual structure (the King vs. the Shreshtha fault line, DYN-01 §3) + the Stress (the overstretch, the fiscal strain, the peasant revolt, the famine, the epidemic — the king's weakness); (immediate) a **weak king** rises (the "most worthy" is weak, the "eldest" is passed over), the Shreshtha dominates (the Maratha Peshwa logic).
- **Actors:** the King (the weak king, the "eldest" or the "most worthy"), the Shreshtha (the dominance, the power), the Veshna matha (the sanction, the kingmaker — the split), the chiefs (the accumulation of power, the regionalization), the merchant/bankers (the war finance, the debt).
- **Immediate effects:** the **Shreshtha dominates** (the Maratha Peshwa logic) — the weak king is a figurehead, the Shreshtha controls the army, the revenue, the administration; the **Veshna matha is split** (the sanction is withheld — the kingmaker is divided); the **chiefs accumulate power** (the regionalization, the successor-state incubator).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the Shreshtha (the dominance, the power), the chiefs (the accumulation of power, the regionalization). Losers — the King (the weak king, the figurehead), the Veshna matha (the split, the sanction is withheld).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **Shreshtha's dominance** (the Maratha Peshwa logic — the court is the Shreshtha's, the King is a figurehead); the **Veshna matha is split** (the sanction is withheld — the kingmaker is divided, the Great Succession Crisis is set up); the **chiefs' accumulation of power** (the regionalization — the successor-state incubator); the **weak king** (the "eldest" or the "most worthy" — the Great Succession Crisis is set up).

## Era 5 — The Fragmentation (500–600+)

### KE ~500–560 — The Phre's Arrival (the chartered company, the delta enclaves)

- **Causes:** (root) the Phre's naval/maritime power (the island, the British logic, FOR-01 §4) + the Voren (the maritime hub, the economic center of gravity, GEO-02) + the Stress (the Samur's weakness, the Shreshtha's dominance, the Veshna split); (immediate) the Phre projects through a **chartered trading company** (the state-within-a-state, the externalized cost of empire).
- **Actors:** the Phre (the chartered company, the naval logistics), the Voren (the port, the delta mouth), the merchant/bankers (the shahukar, the trade, the debt), the Shreshtha (the dominance, the weakness), the Vethra (the southern rivalry — the Vethra and the Phre compete for the south).
- **Immediate effects:** the Phre establishes its **first enclave at Voren** (the delta mouth, the economic center of gravity, the reverse-invasion axis); the Phre **captures the maritime trade** (Voren — the pepper, the textiles, the silver); the **economic center of gravity shifts** (the Voren, the Phre — the Samur's trade is captured).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the Phre (the chartered company, the delta mouth, the maritime trade). Losers — the Samur (the maritime trade, the Voren, the economic center of gravity), the merchant/bankers (the trade, the debt — the Phre's capture of Voren).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **Phre's power base** (the delta mouth, the reverse-invasion axis — the Phre's leverage); the **economic center of gravity shifts** (the Voren, the Phre — the Samur's trade is captured); the **Phre's paramountcy** (the indirect rule, the subsidiary alliance — the vassals, the successor states); the **Phre's capture of Voren** (the **economic trigger** of the Fragmentation, TIM-01).

### KE ~520–560 — The Great Succession Crisis (the trigger)

- **Causes:** (root) the succession system (the three-way contest — the house, the Shreshtha, the temple, DYN-01 §4) + the Shreshtha's dominance (the weak king, the Maratha Peshwa logic, KE ~480–500) + the Veshna matha split (the sanction is withheld, the kingmaker is divided, KE ~480–500) + the Stress (the overstretch, the fiscal strain, the peasant revolt, the famine, the epidemic — the king's weakness); (immediate) a **king dies without a clear "most worthy" heir** (the "eldest" is weak, a "most worthy" younger agnate is backed by the Shreshtha, the temple is split).
- **Actors:** the royal house (the agnates — the "eldest," the "most worthy"), the Shreshtha (the preference, the dominance), the Veshna matha (the split, the sanction is withheld), the chiefs (the accumulation of power, the regionalization — they back different claimants), the Phre (the delta mouth, the leverage — they back the claimant who serves their interest).
- **Immediate effects:** the **Great Succession Crisis** (the contested succession) — the empire is **divided** among the claimants (the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the bār core + the Khor north); the **composite monarchy begins to collapse** (the Mughal 1707 pattern); the **chiefs back different claimants** (the regionalization, the successor-state incubator).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the Shreshtha (the preference, the dominance — the claimant they back), the chiefs (the accumulation of power, the regionalization — they back the claimant who serves their interest), the Phre (the leverage — the claimant who serves their interest). Losers — the royal house (the agnates — the "eldest" and the "most worthy" are divided), the Veshna matha (the split, the sanction is withheld — the kingmaker is divided).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **empire is divided** (the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the bār core + the Khor north — the successor states); the **composite monarchy collapses** (the Mughal 1707 pattern — the fragmentation); the **chiefs' regionalization** (the successor-state incubator — the successor states); the **Phre's leverage** (the claimant who serves their interest — the Phre's paramountcy); the **Veshna matha is divided** (the sanction is withheld — the kingmaker is divided, the religious fault line).

### KE ~550–650 — The Vethra's Rise (the dominant southern power)

- **Causes:** (root) the Vethra's "state too big" (the compact, irrigated, craft-rich southern state, FOR-01 §2) + the Samur's fragmentation (the Great Succession Crisis, the Shreshtha's dominance, the Veshna split — the Samur's weakness); (immediate) the Samur's southern drain (the Vethra wars, the containment) is broken — the Vethra rises.
- **Actors:** the Vethra (the Veth court, the craft-guilds, the Veth faith), the Samur (the fragmented — the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the bār core), the Phre (the delta mouth, the leverage — the Vethra and the Phre compete for the south).
- **Immediate effects:** the **Vethra rises** (the "state too big" becomes the **dominant southern power**) — the Vethra's southern drain (the Vethra wars, the containment) is broken; the **Vethra and the Phre compete for the south** (the Vethra's rise + the Phre's delta — the post-imperial rivalry).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the Vethra (the rise, the dominant southern power). Losers — the Samur (the southern drain, the fragmentation — the Vethra's rise is a loss), the Phre (the delta mouth, the leverage — the Vethra's rise is a rival).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **Vethra is the dominant southern power** (the post-imperial — the Vethra's rise is the long-term geopolitical consequence); the **Vethra and the Phre compete for the south** (the post-imperial rivalry — the Vethra's rise + the Phre's delta); the **Samur's fragmentation is complete** (the Vethra's rise is the final blow — the Samur's southern drain is broken).

### KE ~600–700 — The Successor States + The End of the House of Kesra (the Fragmentation)

- **Causes:** (root) the Great Succession Crisis (the empire is divided, KE ~520–560) + the Vethra's rise (the dominant southern power, KE ~550–650) + the Phre's paramountcy (the delta mouth, the leverage, KE ~500–560) + the chiefs' regionalization (the successor-state incubator, KE ~340–380); (immediate) the **successor states** emerge (the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the bār core + the Khor north), the **House of Kesra ends** (~KE 650–700).
- **Actors:** the chiefs (the successor states — the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the bār core), the Shreshtha (the dominance — the bār core + the Khor north), the Veshna matha (the divided — the sanction is withheld, the religious fault line), the Phre (the paramountcy — the delta mouth, the vassals), the Vethra (the dominant southern power — the rival).
- **Immediate effects:** the **successor states** emerge (the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the bār core + the Khor north — the composite monarchy collapses, the Mughal 1707 pattern); the **House of Kesra ends** (~KE 650–700 — the dynasty is deposed/fragmented); the **Veshna matha survives** (the religious institution — the mobile node, the post-imperial); the **Phre's paramountcy** (the delta mouth, the vassals — the indirect rule, the subsidiary alliance); the **Vethra is the dominant southern power** (the post-imperial).
- **Winners/losers:** Winners — the chiefs (the successor states — the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the bār core), the Phre (the paramountcy — the delta mouth, the vassals), the Vethra (the dominant southern power), the Veshna matha (the religious institution — the mobile node, the post-imperial). Losers — the House of Kesra (the dynasty — the end, the deposition/fragmentation), the Shreshtha (the dominance — the bār core + the Khor north, the fragmentation).
- **Second-order consequences (constrain the next generation):** the **successor states** (the Tarn east, the Sareth west, the bār core + the Khor north — the post-imperial); the **House of Kesra ends** (~KE 650–700 — the dynasty is deposed/fragmented, the "loss" narrative, CUL-01 §5); the **Veshna matha survives** (the religious institution — the mobile node, the post-imperial); the **Phre's paramountcy** (the delta mouth, the vassals — the indirect rule, the subsidiary alliance); the **Vethra is the dominant southern power** (the post-imperial — the Vethra's rise is the long-term geopolitical consequence).
