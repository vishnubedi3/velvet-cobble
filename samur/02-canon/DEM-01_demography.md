> **Plausible ranges, not false precision.** The figures are *ranges derived from the climate/soil/logic*, calibrated to the Mughal/Qing agrarian scale. They are **estimates for internal consistency**, not claims of historical accuracy.

## 1. Total population

- **The Samur Empire at its height: ~25–40 million** **[range, not false precision]** — a large agrarian empire, consistent with the great-river-plain + periphery scale.
- **Breakdown by region (rough shares):**
  - **The Middle Oren + the Samur (the bār core): ~45–50%** (the densest, the fiscal heartland).
  - **The Oren Delta + the Veth Shore (the pell coast): ~25–30%** (the dense paddy, the trade).
  - **The Tarn Marches (the east periphery): ~15–20%** (the paddy plain).
  - **The Sareth Marches + the Khor Frontier (the borders): ~5–10%** (the thin mountain/steppe).

## 2. Density (by zone)

| Zone | Density (per sq. league) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| the bār core (the Middle Oren) | ~60–150 | the irrigated grain belt; the densest settled zone |
| the pell coast (the delta + the Veth) | ~80–200 | the wet paddy; densest in the best paddy |
| the Tarn plain (the east) | ~60–120 | the paddy plain, but remote |
| the Sareth Marches (the west) | ~5–30 | the mountain foothill terraces |
| the Khor Frontier (the north) | ~5–20 | the pastoral steppe (the sparsest) |

**The key fact:** the **population is concentrated in the two agrarian zones** (the bār core + the pell coast), which hold **~70–80% of the people on ~50% of the land**. The borders (the Sareth, the Khor) are **thin** — so the empire's **border military presence is a small, expensive elite**, while the **tax base is the dense agrarian core**. This is the **fiscal-military geometry**: a small garrisoned border defending a large, dense, taxable core.

## 3. Migration

- **The main corridor: the Oren.** People (and grain, goods, soldiers, ideas, disease) move **down the Oren** from the core to the delta — the river is the **highway**. The **urban and commercial population** is concentrated along it (Besra, Voren).
- **The secondary corridors:** the **Sareth passes** (west: iron/timber/merchants) and the **Khor frontier** (north: the horse trade, the seasonal pastoral movement).
- **The pastoral mobility (the Khor):** the Khor horse-people are **seasonally mobile** (the steppe pasture) — a **floating population** that is hard to count or tax (a constant friction with the settled empire).
- **The internal labor migration:** seasonal movement within the agrarian zones (the harvest, the irrigation works) — the **labor supply** the military and the fiscal system draw on.

## 4. The epidemic / famine cycle (tied to Q-004)

- **The failed wind (Q-004):** a weak or failed seasonal wind → a **poor pell harvest** (the paddy is the most wind-dependent) → **famine** in the pell zones (the delta, the Tarn, the Veth) → **debt** (the peasant borrows to survive) → **revolt** (when the tax is still demanded). This is the **famine → debt → revolt chain** — the social expression of the monetization chain (GEO-01 §8.4).
- **The epidemic:** the **dense agrarian core + the Oren highway + the Voren port** create the conditions for **epidemic** (the disease travels the river and the sea). A **major epidemic** ("plague" event) is a **once-in-a-generation** shock that can **break a dynasty's legitimacy** and **empty the fields** (the labor supply).
- **The frequency:** a **failed wind / famine every ~5–15 years** (a plausible pre-industrial range), a **catastrophic failure once in a generation or two**; a **major epidemic once in a generation or two** (to be dated on the TIM timeline).
- **The long-run effect:** the **population is not steady** — it **grows in the good-wind decades** and **contracts in the failed-wind / epidemic decades**. The **labor supply** (what the army and the tax depend on) is therefore **cyclical, not fixed**.

## 5. The labor supply (what the army and the tax draw on)

- **The army** draws on the **dense agrarian core** (the bār + the pell) — the **peasant-soldier** base (the majority) + the **border elite** (the garrison, the horse-people, the coastal marines). The **recruit pool** is the **~70–80% in the agrarian zones**.
- **The tax** draws on the **bār core** (the grain) + the **pell coast** (the trade, the pepper) + the **Tarn** (the tribute). The **labor for the irrigation works, the roads, and the garrisons** is the **agrarian surplus labor** (the forced labor / corvée — a standing source of resentment).
- **The key constraint:** the **labor supply is concentrated and cyclical** — the army and the tax both depend on the **same dense, wind-dependent agrarian core**. A **failed wind** or an **epidemic** hits the **labor supply** first, and the **fiscal-military system breaks when the labor breaks.** (A root cause of the Phase 5 decline, interacting with the GEO-01 three-front overstretch.)

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the **population ~25–40 million**; **concentrated in the bār core + the pell coast** (~70–80%); the **borders thin** (a small, expensive garrison); the **labor supply cyclical** (the wind + the epidemic).
- **Unintended:** the **fiscal-military geometry** (a small garrisoned border defending a large, dense, taxable core) makes the empire **vulnerable at the border** (the garrison is small) *and* **vulnerable at the core** (the labor is cyclical); the **famine → debt → revolt chain** is a **standing social fault line**.
- **Long-term:** the **demographic cycle** (the good-wind growth, the failed-wind / epidemic contraction) is a **root cause** of the Phase 5 decline, interacting with the **fiscal-military overstretch** and the **elite competition**. The empire's **military and fiscal power rise and fall with the wind.**
