> FOR-01 is the **foreign powers** (the Vethra, the Khor, the Phre) — each with its own **independent history, institutions, and strategic interests** (not just a relationship to the Samur). The **geopolitical dynamics** (the relationship between them + the Samur) are the last section.

## 1. The geopolitical structure

The Samur Empire (the bār core + the four borders) is surrounded by **three foreign powers**:

- **The Vethra** (the south, the "state too strong for its size," the rivalry).
- **The Khor** (the north, the horse-people, the strategic lifeline).
- **The Phre** (the maritime, the chartered company, the delta, the Fragmentation).

## 2. The Vethra (the "state too strong for its size")

### 2.1 The independent history

- **The founding** — the Vethra is a **compact, well-irrigated, craft-rich** state on the **Veth shore** (the southern coast). Its founding is the **consolidation of the Veth coast** (the pepper, the irrigation, the craft-guilds) — a **court-driven, craft-guild** state (the Mysore logic).
- **The rise** — the Vethra rises as a **compact, irrigated, craft-rich** state — the **pepper** (the high-value coastal good) + the **irrigation** (the wet-rice, the pell) + the **craft-guilds** (the silk, the steel, the textiles) — the **economic engine** (the court as the market anchor, the Mysore logic). The Vethra is **too big for its size** — its military/economic capability exceeds what the Samur (and others) will tolerate.
- **The rivalry with the Samur** — the **first Vethra war** (~KE 200–240, TIM-01) — the **first containment** — the Samur tries to contain the Vethra (the "state too big"). The **Vethra wars** recur (the High Empire + the Stress). The **Vethra rises** (the Fragmentation) — the "state too big" becomes the **dominant southern power** (the Mysore pattern, but here the Vethra *survives* and rises).

### 2.2 The institutions

- **The Veth court** (the economic engine, the market anchor) — the Vethra's state is **court-driven** (the Mysore logic).
- **The Veth faith** (the southern religion, REL-01 §5) — the Vethra's own temple/faith (the rival to the Veshna faith).
- **The craft-guilds** (the silk, the steel, the textiles) — the Vethra's industrialization (the Mysore logic).
- **The irrigation** (the wet-rice, the pell) — the Vethra's agricultural base.

### 2.3 The strategic interests

- **The pepper** (the high-value coastal good) — the Vethra's economic lifeline.
- **The Veth coast** (the ports, the maritime) — the Vethra's trade.
- **The containment** (the rivalry with the Samur) — the Vethra resists the Samur's containment (the "state too big").
- **The rise** (the Fragmentation) — the Vethra becomes the dominant southern power (the post-imperial).

## 3. The Khor (the horse-people)

### 3.1 The independent history

- **The structure (resolves Q-002)** — the Khor is **multiple horse peoples** (the steppe) with a **loose seasonal assembly** (not a single confederation, but multiple competing horse peoples). The Q-002 best guess (multiple horse peoples, loose seasonal assembly) is now canon.
- **The founding** — the Khor is a **nomadic/pastoral** steppe people (the northern frontier). The Khor's founding is the **seasonal assembly** (the horse peoples gather seasonally for the trade, the raiding, the marriage).
- **The horse trade** — the Khor is the **source of the horses** (the strategic lifeline, GEO-01 §5, ECO-01 §3, MIL-01 §3). The Khor's **horse trade** with the Samur (the horses in, the silver/salt/bār out) is the **strategic lifeline**.
- **The Khor faith** — the **steppe religion** (the ancestral/shamanic, the mobile, REL-01 §5) — the Khor's own faith (the rival to the Veshna faith).

### 3.2 The institutions

- **The seasonal assembly** (the loose confederation of the horse peoples) — the Khor's political structure.
- **The horse trade** (the strategic lifeline) — the Khor's economic base.
- **The Khor faith** (the steppe religion) — the Khor's religion.
- **The pastoral mobility** (the seasonal, the steppe pasture) — the Khor's way of life.

### 3.3 The strategic interests

- **The horses** (the strategic lifeline) — the Khor's leverage over the Samur (the horse dependency).
- **The salt/silver** (the Khor highlands, the salt flats) — the Khor's resources.
- **The bār** (the Samur grain, the trade) — the Khor's food.
- **The seasonal assembly** (the trade, the raiding, the marriage) — the Khor's political/economic cycle.
- **The hard frontier** (defensible only by presence, MIL-01 §6) — the Khor's military stance.

## 4. The Phre (the distant maritime power)

### 4.1 The independent history

- **The founding** — the Phre is a **distant maritime power** (the island, the British logic) — a **naval** power (no land frontier, the naval logistics as the existential condition). The Phre's founding is the **naval/maritime** (the sea, the trade, the chartered company).
- **The chartered company (resolves Q-018)** — the Phre projects through a **chartered trading company** (the private monopoly with the right to wage war, make treaties, and collect revenue — the British EIC logic). The **chartered company** is the Phre's **state-within-a-state** (the externalized cost of empire).
- **The delta enclaves** — the Phre establishes its **first enclave at Voren** (the delta mouth, ~KE 500–560, TIM-01) — the **economic center of gravity** (GEO-02) and the **reverse-invasion axis** (GEO-01 §2). The Phre **captures the maritime trade** (Voren) — the **economic trigger** of the fragmentation (TIM-01).
- **The Phre faith/language** — the **port faith** (the maritime, REL-01 §5) + the **Phre language** (the maritime, CUL-01 §2) — the Phre's religion/language.

### 4.2 The institutions

- **The chartered company** (the state-within-a-state, the externalized cost of empire) — the Phre's projection.
- **The naval logistics** (the sea, the sea-lanes, the delta mouth) — the Phre's binding constraint.
- **The paramountcy** (the indirect rule, the subsidiary alliance) — the Phre's control of the vassals.
- **The Phre faith/language** (the port faith, the maritime) — the Phre's religion/language.

### 4.3 The strategic interests

- **The delta mouth** (Voren) — the Phre's power base (the reverse-invasion axis).
- **The maritime trade** (the pepper, the textiles, the silver) — the Phre's economic base.
- **The paramountcy** (the vassals, the successor states) — the Phre's control.
- **The naval logistics** (the sea-lanes, the binding constraint) — the Phre's reach.

## 5. The geopolitical dynamics (the relationship between them + the Samur)

- **Samur vs. Vethra** — the **containment rivalry** (the "state too big") — the Vethra wars.
- **Samur + Khor** — the **horse trade** (the strategic lifeline) + the **hard frontier** (the defense).
- **Samur vs. Phre** — the **delta** (the Phre's capture of Voren) — the **Fragmentation** (the economic trigger).
- **Vethra + Phre** — the **southern rivalry** (the Vethra's rise + the Phre's delta) — the **post-imperial** (the Vethra and the Phre compete for the south).
- **Khor + Phre** — the **steppe + the maritime** (the Khor's horses + the Phre's naval) — the **interaction** (the Khor's leverage + the Phre's reach).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the three foreign powers (the Vethra, the Khor, the Phre) — each with its own independent history, institutions, and strategic interests; the geopolitical dynamics (the relationship between them + the Samur).
- **Unintended:** the **Vethra's rise** (the "state too big" becomes the dominant southern power — the post-imperial); the **Khor's horse dependency** (the Samur's strategic vulnerability — the horse trade); the **Phre's capture of Voren** (the economic trigger of the fragmentation — the delta mouth).
- **Long-term:** the **Vethra** (the dominant southern power, the post-imperial); the **Khor** (the horse-people, the steppe, the strategic lifeline); the **Phre** (the maritime power, the chartered company, the delta, the Fragmentation) — the **three foreign powers** are the **geopolitical context** of the Samur's rise, stress, and fragmentation (TIM-01).
