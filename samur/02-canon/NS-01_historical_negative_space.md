> NS-01 is the **historical negative space** (the Phase 5 requirement) — **why certain things did not happen**, when it provides **explanatory value**. The negative space is the "what didn't happen" that explains the structure. Each entry: **what did not happen**, **why** (the structural reason), and the **explanatory value** (what it explains).

**The negative-space rule:** a negative space is recorded when the *non*-event explains the structure (why the world is what it is). The non-event is as load-bearing as the event.

## 1. Why the Vethra was never conquered (not just contained)

- **What did not happen:** the Vethra (the "state too strong for its size") was **never conquered/annexed** by the Samur — it was **repeatedly contained** (the Vethra wars, TIM-02), but **not destroyed**.
- **Why:** the Vethra is **compact, well-irrigated, craft-rich, and coastal** — the **cost of holding** it (the conquest without integration, the Mughal Deccan logic, MIL-01 §8) **exceeds the revenue of holding** it. The Samur **contains** it (the tribute, the rivalry, the Vethra wars), but does **not annex** it (the **integration cost** — the Vethra is too small to be worth the conquest, but too strong to be ignored).
- **Explanatory value:** explains the **Vethra's survival + rise** (the "state too big" becomes the **dominant southern power** in the Fragmentation, TIM-02 — the Vethra's rise is the long-term geopolitical consequence).

## 2. Why the Khor was never settled / annexed

- **What did not happen:** the Khor (the horse-people) was **never incorporated/settled** by the Samur — it was **traded with** (the horses) and **garrisoned against** (Keshkhor), but **not settled**.
- **Why:** the Khor is **pastoral and mobile** (the seasonal assembly, FOR-01 §3) — the **cost of holding** the steppe (**defensible only by presence**, MIL-01 §6) **exceeds the revenue** (the steppe is **thin**, the DEM-01 density is low). The Samur **trades** (the horses, the strategic lifeline) and **garrisons** (Keshkhor, the hard frontier), but does **not settle** (the **integration cost** — the steppe is too thin to be worth the conquest).
- **Explanatory value:** explains the **Khor's persistence** as an independent power (the **strategic lifeline** + the **hard frontier** — the **Khor horse dependency** is never overcome, MIL-01 §4).

## 3. Why the Tarn tributary was never directly administered

- **What did not happen:** the Tarn (the east) was **never directly administered** by the Samur — it was **held by tribute** (not direct administration), even at the Samur's peak (the High Empire).
- **Why:** the Tarn is **water-crossed** (the paddy plain), **remote**, and **rich** — the **cost of direct administration** (the projection, the garrison, MIL-01 §6) **exceeds the revenue of tribute** (the **tribute without administration**, the Maratha chauth logic, ADM-01 §2). The Samur **extracts** (the tribute), but does **not administer** (the **integration cost** — the Tarn is too remote and water-crossed to be worth the direct administration).
- **Explanatory value:** explains the **Tarn's persistence** as a tributary (the **fragmentation site** — the successor states emerge from the Tarn, TIM-02 — the chiefs' regionalization).

## 4. Why the artillery never displaced the cavalry

- **What did not happen:** the artillery (the guns, the rockets, TEC-01 §4) **never displaced** the cavalry (the Khor horses, the mobile striking force, MIL-01 §3) — the **cavalry remains the load-bearing force**.
- **Why:** the **Khor horse dependency** (the strategic vulnerability, MIL-01 §4) + the **cavalry is mobile** (the steppe, the frontier) + the **artillery is heavy** (the garrison is small, the supply line, MIL-01 §6). The **cavalry** is the **mobile striking force** (the load-bearing), and the **artillery** is a **supplement** (the siege, the fort — the modernizing force, not the striking force).
- **Explanatory value:** explains the **persistence of the cavalry** (the **Khor dependency** is never overcome — the **strategic vulnerability** persists, MIL-01 §4 — the Samur's mobile striking force is tied to the Khor).

## 5. Why the Phre never conquered the interior

- **What did not happen:** the Phre captured Voren (the delta mouth), but **never conquered the interior** (the bār core) — the Phre's reach is **bounded to the delta** (the maritime).
- **Why:** the Phre's reach is **bounded by the riverine reach** (the delta mouth, not the interior — the Oren is the Samur's logistics spine, the reverse-invasion is costly, GEO-01 §2) + the Phre's **chartered company logic** (the **trade, not the territory** — the externalized cost of empire, FOR-01 §4). The Phre **controls the delta** (the maritime), but does **not conquer the interior** (the **integration cost** + the **riverine-reach bound**).
- **Explanatory value:** explains the **Phre's bounded reach** (the delta, not the interior — the Phre is a **completing trigger, not a causal root**, TIM-01 — the Phre captures the economic center of gravity, but does not conquer the empire).

## 6. Why the Veshna matha never became a theocracy

- **What did not happen:** the Veshna matha (the sanctioner, the kingmaker, REL-01 §2) **never became a theocracy** (the rule by the priests) — the matha **sanctions, but does not rule**.
- **Why:** the matha is a **sanctioner** (the consecration, the de-legitimation), **not a ruler** (the army, the revenue) + the **dual structure** (the King + the Shreshtha — the matha is the **third**, the sanctioner, not the ruler, DYN-01 §3) + the **orthodoxy vs. the revival** (the internal disputes — the matha is **divided**, REL-01 §4). The matha **sanctions** (the legitimation), but does **not rule** (the power).
- **Explanatory value:** explains the **matha's persistence** as a sanctioner (not a theocracy — the **religious fault line** persists, but the matha never rules — the kingmaker is a check on the king, not a replacement for him).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the **six negative spaces** (the Vethra never conquered, the Khor never settled, the Tarn never directly administered, the artillery never displaced the cavalry, the Phre never conquered the interior, the matha never became a theocracy) — each with its **what did not happen**, **why** (the structural reason), and **explanatory value**.
- **Unintended:** the **negative-space rule** (the non-event is as load-bearing as the event — the non-event explains the structure); the **integration cost** (the cost of holding exceeds the revenue of holding) is the **recurring reason** for the negative spaces (the Vethra, the Khor, the Tarn, the Phre, the matha).
- **Long-term:** the **negative space** is the **"what didn't happen" texture** of the later narrative (if authorized) — the **Vethra's survival + rise**, the **Khor's persistence** (the horse dependency), the **Tarn's persistence** (the fragmentation site), the **cavalry's persistence** (the Khor dependency), the **Phre's bounded reach** (the delta, not the interior), the **matha's persistence** (the sanctioner, not the theocracy) — the **negative space** is the **explanatory backbone** of the structure.
