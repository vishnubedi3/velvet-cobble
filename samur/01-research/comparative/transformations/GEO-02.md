# Transformation Log — GEO-02 (Cities, Regions, Borders)

Date: 2026-08-26
Canon target: **GEO-02** (the seven regions; the eight cities; the three Sareth passes; the four border kinds)
Method: the 5-step sequential reasoning.

## Step 1 — Historical examples and underlying mechanisms

- **Maratha** — the **pass fortress**: a chokepoint town where the defense is stationed and the border trade runs.
- **Mughal** — the **river capital** (the great river + the fiscal base) and the **delta port** (the mouth as the trade chokepoint).
- **Mysore / Vijayanagara** — the **court city** (the capital as economic engine) and the **temple city** (the temple economy as fiscal sink + legitimation).
- **Qing** — the **frontier garrison + emporium** at the steppe edge (the presence that makes the open frontier manageable).
- **All models** — the **political-vs-economic center tension** (the capital in the fiscal core; the trade wealth at the coast).

## Step 2 — Original historical conditions

- The Maratha Sahyadri passes (the defensible chokepoints).
- The Mughal great river + the Gangetic plain (the river capital; the delta as the trade end).
- The Mysore court (the economic engine) + the Hindu temple economy.
- The Qing steppe edge (the garrison + the trade emporium at the frontier).

## Step 3 — Equivalent or contrasting Samur conditions

- **Kesra** (the capital) sits at the **river + the mountain** (the Oren + the Sareth) in the **fiscal heartland** — analogous to the Mughal river capital, *but* the **economic/maritime center is a separate city (Voren, the delta)**, not the capital.
- **Threna** (the pass fortress) is analogous to the Maratha pass town, but the Sareth is a **west wall with three passes** (the defense and the trade use *different* passes), not one range with one dominant route.
- **Keshkhor** (the horse emporium) is analogous to the Qing frontier garrison + emporium, but the Khor is **open steppe (no wall)** — the garrison is a *presence + a trade emporium*, not a walled frontier city.
- **Veshna** (the temple city) is analogous to the temple-economy anchor, but it is a **separate city from the capital** — a religious counterweight, not co-located with the court.
- **Contrasts (the load-bearing differences):** the **political capital (Kesra) and the economic/maritime hub (Voren) are separated by the length of the Oren** — the empire's **economic center of gravity sits at the coast, not at the capital**; and the **borders are four different kinds** (wall / open steppe / tribute plain / contested coast), requiring four different border strategies.

## Step 4 — Original Samur solution

The specific layout — **Kesra** (capital at the river + the mountain, in the bār core), **Veshna** (temple city), **Besra** (grain market), **Threna** (main-pass fortress), **Gheshar** (second-pass waystation), **Keshkhor** (horse emporium), **Voren** (delta port), **Kesveth** (Veth port); the **seven regions** under **direct / garrison / tribute (+ port) control**; the **three Sareth passes** with defense and trade on different routes — is the **Samur-specific answer** to the **Samur geography** (the Oren spine, the Sareth wall, the Khor steppe, the Veth coast, the Tarn plain). It is **not a reskin**: the **separation of the political capital from the economic/maritime hub** (Kesra ≠ Voren) and the **four-kind border** are the load-bearing differences.

## Step 5 — Consequences

- **Immediate:** the political center (Kesra), the fiscal hub (Besra), the maritime hub (Voren), the western defense (Threna), the northern horse trade (Keshkhor), the southern pepper interest (Kesveth) are fixed.
- **Unintended:** the **political-vs-economic tension** (Kesra vs. Voren) grows as the coast richens; the **direct/garrison/tribute control** makes the empire **uneven** (strong core, weak periphery) — the fragmentation site.
- **Long-term:** **Voren** becomes the **Phre's power-base** (the reverse-invasion axis); the **Tarn Marches** become the **successor-state incubator**; the **Kesra–Voren axis** is the empire's strategic spine and its greatest vulnerability.
