> ADM-01 is the administration as a **structure** (the central/provincial government, the revenue, the legal pluralism, the infrastructure). The *specific* offices, officials, rates, and codes are the later ADM/ECO files.

## 1. The central government (the Kesra court)

The **court at Kesra** is the **state's center** (the Mughal court logic — "where the Shreshtha and the king are, the state is"). The **five factions** (DYN-01 §5) are all represented at the court:

- **The king (the House of Kesra)** — the symbolic/ritual center, the protector of dharma.
- **The Shreshtha** — the head of the bureaucracy; the army, the revenue, the administration.
- **The temple (the Veshna matha)** — the sanctioner, the consecration, the religious law.
- **The chiefs** — the representatives of the regional power base (the hereditary districts).
- **The merchant/bankers** — the war financiers (the shahukar), the urban/commercial power.

**The central offices** (all **salaried, non-hereditary** — the Mughal mansab logic; the holder depends on the king/Shreshtha's continuous favor):

1. **The Shreshtha** — the prime minister/regent; the top of the bureaucracy; the army, the revenue, the administration.
2. **The Revenue Head** — the assessment of the bār grain, the pell, the silver; the collection; the tax targets.
3. **The Military Head** — the recruitment, the garrisons, the campaigns, the frontier generals.
4. **The Temple (the Veshna matha)** — the consecration, the legitimation, the ritual/moral law, the kingmaker.
5. **The Court** — the king's household, the patronage, the legitimation.

**The dual elite in the bureaucracy (DYN-01 §5, the Qing banner/li-bu logic):** the **top offices** (the Shreshtha, the Military Head) are held by the **founding elite** (the Samur warrior band), while the **lower/revenue offices** (the clerks, the assessors) are held by the **absorbed elite** (the co-opted professional clerks — the Vijayanagara Vellala logic). **The conqueror's class supervises the co-opted class** (the Qing dou logic).

## 2. The provincial government (the tiered control, GEO-02 §1)

The provinces are administered in **four ways**, matched to the geography:

1. **The bār core (the Samur + the Middle Oren) — directly administered.** The Shreshtha's bureaucracy extends to the district; the Revenue Head assesses the bār grain; the clerks collect. The **fiscal heartland** — the most directly controlled.
2. **The Sareth Marches + the Khor Frontier (the hard borders) — garrisoned.** The Military Head controls the garrisons (Threna, Keshkhor); the **frontier generals** (the Qing fuheng logic) have **wide autonomy at the frontier** (the tiered sovereignty). The passes, the frontier trade.
3. **The Tarn Marches (the tributary east) — held by tribute.** The local chiefs (the absorbed nayaka) collect the **tribute** (the chauth/sardeshmukhi logic — **tribute without administration**); the empire does not directly administer, it extracts the tribute. The chiefs keep their districts (hereditary in practice).
4. **The Oren Delta + the Veth Shore (the coast) — traded/port-controlled.** The port officials (the Revenue Head) control the delta mouth (Voren), the pepper trade, the **transit/market dues**; the coast is the trade wealth.

**The tiered sovereignty** (the Qing logic) is by **geography/administrative cost**, **not** by ethnicity — the empire rules its regions differently *because the geography demands it* (GEO-02 §1).

## 3. The revenue (the monetization chain, GEO-01 §8.4)

- **The bār core:** the **zabt logic** — periodic land measurement/assessment; payment in **grain (the bār)** and, increasingly, in **silver (cash)**; the **tax target** set by the Revenue Head.
- **The pell coast:** the paddy is taxed (the pell); the **trade goods** (the pepper) are taxed (the transit/market dues).
- **The state monopoly** (the Mughal logic): the state monopolizes the key trades (the **salt**, the **horses**, the **pepper**); the **textiles** are **taxed, not monopolized** (the guilds produce; the state takes the transit dues + the court purchase) — the **transit/market dues** — the state is the **largest purchaser/employer**. *(Canon fix 2026-08-28 [CC-04]: the monopoly list corrected per the ECO-01 §4 + ECO-02 §2 (the Q-023 resolution) — the textiles are taxed, not monopolized.)*
- **The monetization chain:** the push for **cash (silver)** forces **market integration** — the peasant must sell grain/cash crops to pay the **silver tax** → the **moneylender** (the shahukar/merchant-banker) → the **peasant debt** → the **revolt when the targets inflate** (the Mughal logic). This is the **fiscal engine** and the **social fault line** (DEM-01, TIM-01).

## 4. The currency (the silver standard)

- The **silver** (GEO-01 §5 — from the Khor + the Sareth) is the **monetary metal**. The empire mints **silver coins** (the currency) + uses **grain** as the **tax-in-kind** (the bār). So there is a **dual currency** (the silver coin + the grain) — the **monetization** is the push from the grain-in-kind to the silver-cash (the Mughal zabt cash-revenue logic).
- The **silver comes from the frontier** (the Khor, the Sareth) — so the **monetization is tied to the frontier trade** (GEO-01 §8.4) — a **failed frontier** (the Khor, the Sareth) is a **monetary crisis** (the silver shortage → the tax crisis).

## 5. The legal pluralism (the dharma-shastra logic)

The empire has a **plural legal order** (the Sanatan dharma-shastra logic) — **four coexisting laws**:

1. **The king's law** — the public order, the state's revenue/military law (the state appoints the judges — the qadi logic).
2. **The jati/corporate law** — the internal law of the occupational castes (the panchayat, the self-governing corporation).
3. **The temple law** — the ritual/moral law (the Veshna matha, the consecration, the legitimation).
4. **The port law** — the maritime/trade law (Voren, the transit/market dues, the Phre).

This is the **bounded legal pluralism** (the Islam madhhab logic) — a plural but bounded order, with the **king's law** as the overarching public order and the **corporate/temple/port laws** as the internal/specialized laws. The **judges** are appointed by the respective authority (the state, the caste, the temple, the port).

## 6. The infrastructure (the state's physical power)

- **The Oren** (the river, the logistics spine) — the **river transport** (the boats, the grain moves down the Oren).
- **The irrigation works** (the bār core, the tanks/anicut — the Mysore/Vijayanagara logic) — the state directs the irrigation (the surplus base, DEM-01).
- **The roads** (the Sareth passes, the Khor frontier, the trade routes) — the state maintains the roads (the frontier, the trade).
- **The garrisons** (Threna, Keshkhor, the passes) — the military infrastructure.
- **The delta port** (Voren) — the maritime infrastructure (the docks, the warehouses, the enclaves — the Phre's later power-base).

## 7. Tax evasion — who benefits and who resists

**Benefits:** the **state** (the revenue), the **Shreshtha** (the power), the **court** (the patronage), the **merchant/bankers** (the trade, the debt), the **port** (the trade wealth).

**Resists (the tax evasion / the revolt):**

1. **The chiefs** (the absorbed nayaka) — the **hereditary-in-practice districts** — they **under-assess, keep the surplus** (the regional power base, the evasion). This is the **chief's resistance** (the regionalization, DYN-01).
2. **The peasants** (the bār core) — when the **targets inflate** (the monetization chain breaks), they **revolt** (the Mughal satnami/Jat logic).
3. **The temples** (the Veshna matha) — the **temple economy** (the land grants, the fiscal sink) — the temple lands are **outside the direct tax** (the parallel landholding, the Vijayanagara/waqf logic) — the temple **evasion** (the tax-exempt institutional property).
4. **The merchant/bankers** (the shahukar) — they **control the monetization** (the debt) and **resist** the state's attempts to control the cash (the growth imperative).
5. **The coast/port** (Voren) — the **trade** is hard to tax (the maritime, the Phre) — the port officials **evasion** (the contested transit/market dues).

**The structural tension:** the **state** (the Shreshtha, the Revenue Head) wants to **maximize the revenue** (the tax targets, the monetization), but the **chiefs** (the evasion), the **peasants** (the revolt), the **temples** (the tax-exempt land), the **merchant/bankers** (the control of the cash), and the **port** (the contested trade) all **resist** — so the **revenue is always contested** (the fiscal-military tension, DEM-01, TIM-01).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the **central government** (the court, the Shreshtha, the offices, the dual elite); the **tiered provincial government** (direct/garrison/tribute/port); the **revenue** (the zabt, the state monopoly, the monetization chain); the **currency** (the silver standard, the dual currency); the **legal pluralism** (the four laws); the **infrastructure** (the Oren, the irrigation, the roads, the garrisons, the port).
- **Unintended:** the **tax evasion is structural** (the chiefs, the peasants, the temples, the merchant/bankers, the port all resist) — the **revenue is always contested** (the fiscal-military tension); the **monetization** (the push for the silver cash) **ties the fiscal engine to the frontier** (the silver from the Khor/Sareth) — a **failed frontier is a monetary crisis**; the **tiered control** makes the empire **uneven** (the direct core, the garrisoned borders, the tributary east, the port coast) — the **site of the later fragmentation**.
- **Long-term:** the **revenue contest** (the state vs. the five resisters) is a **root cause** of the fiscal stress (TIM-01); the **monetization chain** (the silver → the cash → the debt → the revolt) is the **social fault line** (DEM-01); the **tiered control** is the **fragmentation site** (the tributary east → the successor states); the **dual elite** (the founding vs. the absorbed) is the **elite fault line** (DYN-01).
