> TIM-04 establishes the **world's own chronology** (the deep timescale) and places the **Kesra Era (KE 0–680+, the six epochs)** within it. It does **not** copy Earth's chronology, does **not** assume Earth's exact formation history, and does **not** arbitrarily assign a specific deep scale (5,000 years? billions of years?). It establishes only what the established canon's internal causality requires: that there is **pre-imperial history** (the Veshna faith/temple pre-dating KE 0), and that the **Kesra Era is the most recent slice** of a deeper timescale. The **scale of the pre-imperial history is left undetermined** (the established canon does not specify it).

## 1. The Kesra Era's position in the deep timescale

The **Kesra Era (KE 0–680+, the six epochs)** is the **most recent slice** of the world's history. It begins at **KE 0** (the founding, Kesra I's consecration, TIM-01) and runs to the **Dhaneshra Period's equilibrium** (post-KE ~900, TIM-03 §11). The Kesra Era is **not** the complete history of the world or of Samur civilization — it is the **most recent slice** of a deeper timescale.

## 2. The pre-imperial history (before KE 0)

The established canon demonstrates that there is **pre-imperial history** (history before KE 0):
- **DYN-01 §2 #3:** the founding (KE 0) "**co-opted** the Veshna temple, the founding faith's center" — the Veshna faith/temple **pre-dated the founding** (the founding "co-opted" an existing Veshna temple/faith).
- **REL-02 §1:** "The Kesra Charter... attributed to the founding era (KE 0–35)... is the **oldest text** (its orthography is frozen)." — the Kesra Charter (the oldest text) is from the founding era (KE 0–35), but the Veshna faith/temple pre-dated the founding (the founding "co-opted" it).
- **CUL-02 §2:** "The Veshna sacred script — an **older** abugida... It is **conservative** (it changes slower than the Orenic script — the sacred text's orthography is frozen)." — the Veshna sacred script is "older" (older than the Orenic script) and "frozen" (conservative orthography), implying a long, conservative sacred tradition.

So the established canon demonstrates that the **Veshna faith/temple pre-dated the founding (KE 0)**. The **scale of that pre-imperial antiquity is not specified** in the established canon. The **Kesra Charter** (the oldest text) is from the founding era (KE 0–35), but the **Veshna faith/temple pre-dated the founding** (the founding "co-opted" it).

## 3. The deep timescale (the world's own chronology)

The world's own chronology (the deep timescale) follows the **principle of scale**:
- **Cosmic history** is vastly older than **planetary history**.
- **Planetary history** is vastly older than **biological civilization**.
- **Biological history** is vastly older than **organized states**.
- **Civilizational history** is vastly older than the **current imperial period** (the Kesra Era, KE 0–680+).

The **Kesra Era (KE 0–680+)** is the **most recent slice** of the world's history. The **pre-imperial history** (before KE 0) is **vastly older** than the Kesra Era, but its **scale is not specified** in the established canon. The **wider world** (the planet, the wider civilizations, the cosmos) exists on a **vastly older timescale**, but its **scale is not specified** in the established canon.

## 4. What is established vs. undetermined

- **Established:** the Kesra Era (KE 0–680+) is the most recent slice of the world's history; the Veshna faith/temple pre-dated the founding (KE 0); the Kesra Charter (the oldest text) is from the founding era (KE 0–35); the Veshna sacred script is "older" and "frozen" (conservative).
- **Undetermined (left alone until ready):** the **scale of the pre-imperial history** (how much older than KE 0 the Veshna faith/temple is); the **wider world's timescale** (the planet's age, the cosmos's age); the **pre-imperial history** (the history of the Veshna faith/temple before KE 0, the wider civilizations, the cosmology). These are **left alone until they are ready to be resolved** (per the stewardship principle: "If sufficient information does not exist, leave it untouched. Record only the information necessary to preserve the reason it remains unresolved and what would eventually permit a proper resolution.").

## 5. The Kesra Era's position (the correct position from the existing project)

The **Kesra Era (KE 0 to the present, the six epochs, running from KE 0 (the founding) to the Dhaneshra Period's equilibrium (post-KE ~900))** is the **most recent slice** of the world's history. The **existing Kesra Era history (KE 0 to the present, the six epochs)** is **not artificially stretched apart** — it remains KE 0 to the present (the six epochs), unchanged. The **pre-imperial history** (before KE 0) is **vastly older** than the Kesra Era, but its **scale is not specified** in the established canon. The **deep timescale** (the pre-imperial history, the wider world, the cosmology) is **established as existing** (the Veshna faith/temple pre-dating KE 0, the wider world's vastly older timescale), but its **specific scale is left undetermined** (the established canon does not specify it; Q-083/Q-084 are left alone until ready).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the Kesra Era (KE 0–680+) is placed within the world's deep timescale (the most recent slice); the pre-imperial history (before KE 0) is established as existing (the Veshna faith/temple pre-dating KE 0); the wider world's vastly older timescale is established as existing; the scale of the pre-imperial history and the wider world's timescale is left undetermined.
- **Unintended:** the Kesra Era (KE 0–680+) is now explicitly the **most recent slice** of a deeper timescale (not the complete history of the world or of Samur civilization); the pre-imperial history (before KE 0) is now established as existing (the Veshna faith/temple pre-dating KE 0); the wider world's vastly older timescale is now established as existing.
- **Long-term:** the Kesra Era (KE 0–680+) is the **most recent slice** of the world's history; the pre-imperial history (before KE 0) and the wider world's vastly older timescale are **established as existing** but their **scale is left undetermined** (left alone until ready to be resolved).
