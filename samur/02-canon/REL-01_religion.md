> REL-01 is the religion as a **historical system** (theology, institutions, political relationships, internal disputes) — **not** an aesthetic. The main subject is the **Veshna faith** (the founding faith, the dominant); the context is the **religious pluralism** (the other regional strands).

## 1. The Veshna faith — the theology (the grammar)

- The Veshna faith is a **dharma-based** system (the Sanatan logic) — a **grammar**, not a single creed. The core grammar: **dharma** (duty/order, **context-dependent** — by varna, by stage of life, by time and place), **karma** (moral causation across lives), **moksha** (liberation as the telos), and the **varna framework** (the ritual-social grammar, **contested from within**).
- **No single creed, no prophet, no central scripture** — authority is **dispersed** across the **commentarial tradition**, the **guru–śiṣya lineage**, the **temple**, and the **local**. The system is **inherently plural and resists centralization**.
- **The load-bearing difference (the centralization):** the Veshna faith is **institutionally anchored to a single sacred center** — the **Veshna matha** — which **sanctions the king** (the consecration, DYN-01 §3). This is the **institutional innovation**: a **centralized dharma** with a **single sanctioning center** (unlike the dispersed Sanatan authority, where no single center sanctions the king).

## 2. The Veshna faith — the institutions

1. **The Veshna matha** — the **single sacred center** (the founding faith's center, GEO-02 §2 — the temple city of Veshna). The **sanctioner** (the consecration, the de-legitimation, the kingmaker, DYN-01 §3). The **head of the matha** (the guru) is the **high priest** (the kingmaker).
2. **The temples** — the **temple network** (the Veshna temple + the regional temples). The **temple economy** (the land grants, the fiscal sink, the parallel landholding, the Vijayanagara/Sanatan logic) — the temple is an **economic institution** (the employer, the market, the repository, the center of local administration — the Sanatan temple logic).
3. **The priestly class** — the **brāhmanical/priestly** class (the learned in the dharma, the commentarial tradition, the ritual). The **self-reproducing scholarly class** (the Sanatan guru–śiṣya logic + the Islam ulema logic) — a **parallel elite** to the military/nobility.
4. **The guru–śiṣya / the matha** — the **mobile institutional node** (the Sanatan logic) — the matha projects influence across regions **without a territorial state**.

## 3. The Veshna faith — the relationship with politics

- **The matha as the sanctioner** (the consecration, the de-legitimation, the kingmaker) — the **load-bearing** relationship (DYN-01 §3). The matha **sanctions the king** (the ritual that makes a king "legitimate") and can **de-legitimize** (the last resort).
- **The temple economy** (the fiscal sink, the parallel landholding) — the **tax-exempt institutional property** (the Vijayanagara/waqf logic) — the temple lands are **outside the direct tax** (ADM-01 §7 — the temple evasion).
- **The king as protector of dharma** (the Sanatan logic) — the king is the **protector** of the dharma (the legitimation), the **patron** of the matha (the patronage).
- **The matha–court symbiosis (with friction)** — the **matha (the priestly) vs. the court (the Shreshtha/king)** — the **legitimation (the matha) vs. the power (the court)** fault line (DYN-01 §3). When the **court tries to control the matha** (the patronage, the appointments), the **matha resists** (the sanction, the de-legitimation) — the **standing friction** (the Islam ulema–sultan logic).

## 4. The Veshna faith — the internal disputes

- **The orthodoxy vs. the revival** (the Sanatan bhakti logic) — the **standing dynamic** (the temple/priestly/ritual orthodoxy vs. the vernacular/direct/anti-hierarchical revival). The **revival** creates a **vernacular literary culture** + a **popular devotional identity that cuts across varna** — a threat to the orthodoxy and a tool for the state (the popular devotion is easier to patronize).
- **The varna vs. the jāti** (the ideal frame vs. the actual diversity) — the **contested varna framework** (the ritual-social grammar, contested from within).
- **The matha vs. the regional temples** (the central sanctioner vs. the local temples) — the **centralization vs. the local** tension (the matha's authority vs. the regional temples' autonomy).

## 5. The religious pluralism (the other strands)

The Samur empire is **religiously plural** (the multi-regional empire, Q-007):

- **The Veshna faith** — the **founding faith**, the **dominant** (the core, the matha as the sanctioner).
- **The Khor faith** — the **steppe religion** of the horse-people (the north, the mobile, the ancestral/shamanic) — the **Khor** (the northern frontier).
- **The Veth faith** — the **religion of the Vethra** (the "state too strong") — the **southern** religion (the compact state, its own temple/faith) — the **Vethra** (the southern state, the rivalry).
- **The Tarn faiths** — the **religions of the tributary east** (the local, the tributary) — the **Tarn** (the tributary east).
- **The port faith** — the **religion of the Phre** (the maritime) — the **Phre** (the chartered company, the delta, the Fragmentation).

The **religious pluralism** is the **Sanatan mosaic logic** + the **multi-regional empire** (the geography, the regions). The **Veshna faith** is the **dominant** (the founding, the matha), but the **other strands** are the **context** (the pluralism, the coexistence/competition).

## 6. The religious system as a whole (the legal order, the coexistence)

- **The temple law** (the ADM-01 §5 four-law pluralism) — the **ritual/moral law** (the Veshna matha, the consecration, the legitimation) — one of the four coexisting laws (the king's law, the jati law, the temple law, the port law).
- **The religious coexistence/competition** — the **Veshna faith** (the dominant) + the **other strands** (the Khor, the Veth, the Tarn, the port) — the **coexistence** (the pluralism, the tolerance) + the **competition** (the Vethra rivalry, the Phre's capture of the delta).
- **The religious patronage** — the **king/court** patronizes the **Veshna faith** (the matha, the temples) — the **patronage as coalition management** (the Mysore logic) — the court **switches the favored religious bloc** to manage the elite (the patronage oscillation).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the Veshna faith (the dharma grammar, the centralization around the matha, the institutions — the matha/temples/priestly class/mobile node, the relationship with politics — the sanctioner/temple economy/protector of dharma/matha–court friction, the internal disputes — the orthodoxy vs. the revival/varna vs. the jāti/matha vs. the regional temples); the religious pluralism (the other strands — the Khor/Veth/Tarn/port); the religious system as a whole (the temple law, the coexistence/competition, the patronage).
- **Unintended:** the **centralized dharma** (the matha as the single sanctioning center) is the **institutional innovation** (the kingmaker, the target when the court tries to control it); the **temple economy** (the tax-exempt parallel fiscal base) is the **temple evasion** (ADM-01 §7); the **orthodoxy vs. the revival** is the **standing dynamic** (the vernacular culture, the popular devotion); the **matha–court friction** is the **standing fault line** (the legitimation vs. the power).
- **Long-term:** the **matha as the kingmaker** (the sanction, the de-legitimation) is the **succession trigger** (the Great Succession Crisis, TIM-01); the **temple economy** (the tax-exempt land) is the **parallel fiscal base** (the fragmentation site); the **orthodoxy vs. the revival** is the **cultural fault line** (the vernacular, the popular); the **religious pluralism** (the regional strands) is the **post-imperial religious map** (the successor states' religions); the **Phre's port faith** (the maritime) is the **Fragmentation's religious dimension** (the Phre's capture of the delta).
