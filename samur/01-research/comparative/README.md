# Comparative Historical Research Register

## Mandate

Study as historical systems (material conditions, institutions, incentives — not aesthetics): the **Mughal Empire**, the **Vijayanagara Empire**, the **Maratha Confederacy**, the **Kingdom of Mysore**, the **British Empire**, and the **Qing dynasty**.

## Transformation Method (mandatory for every major institution/event)

No real-world institution may be transplanted into canon directly. Every major Samur institution or event passes through five sequential steps:

1. **Historical example and underlying mechanism** — what the institution did, and the material pressure that produced it.
2. **Original historical conditions** — the specific conditions that made it work (population, technology, ecology, rival states, revenue base).
3. **Equivalent or contrasting Samur conditions** — which of those conditions exist in Samur canon, which do not, and which analogous pressures exist that the original never faced.
4. **Original Samur solution** — the institution as a Samur-specific answer to a Samur-specific pressure. It must differ from the original in at least one load-bearing structural way, or the retention must be explicitly justified as independent convergence.
5. **Consequences** — immediate, unintended, and long-term. Recorded as canon consequences or as open questions.

A Samur institution that is merely a **renamed historical counterpart** (no structural difference, no differing conditions) is a redesign candidate and is flagged in the influence register.
