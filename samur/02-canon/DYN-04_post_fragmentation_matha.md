> DYN-04 resolves **Q-079 (the post-fragmentation matha politics)**. It does not answer the question in isolation: it reconstructs the political situation from the established canon (the five eras, the five polities, the houses, the rulers, the temple network, the resources, the established relationships) and constructs the actual political system that develops after the fragmentation. Every major development follows from something already established or from a clearly documented new cause. It establishes the **sixth epoch — the Dhaneshra Period** (post-KE 675, the Fragmentation era's "600+" tail now named and dated). It evolves one fixed institution — the **Veshna matha** — in a historically justified way (the geographic fragmentation forces a conciliar + concordat political system); it does not reopen or casually revise any other established element.

## 1. What "post-fragmentation matha politics" means (the reconstruction from canon)

**What fragmentation occurred?** The Great Succession Crisis (KE 520–555) divided the empire (the de facto division KE 528, the Treaty of Besra KE 555); the successor states formalized (the Tarn Confederation KE 595; the Sareth Dhaneshra KE 610; the Kesra Dhaneshra KE 650); the House of Kesra deposed KE 675 (the rump's dynastic legitimacy ends; the political/economic collapse, NOT the de-consecration — REL-02 §8). The **Vethra** rose (KE 550–620; the Third Vethra War KE 555–560 won the southern fringe). The **Phre** consolidated (the First Enclave Voren KE 510; the Second Enclave KE 530; the paramountcy).

**Which matha political units emerged from it?** The Veshna temple network (REL-02 §4: the Veshna Great Temple apex at Veshna + the 7 Regional Head Temples + the ~250–400 local shrines) is now **geographically split across the five polities**:
- **The Kesra Dhaneshra (the rump)** holds **the Veshna Great Temple (the apex, the guru's seat, at Veshna)** + **3 of the 7 Regional Head Temples** (Kesra, Besra, Keshkhor).
- **The Sareth Dhaneshra** holds **1** (Threna).
- **The Tarn Confederation** holds **1** (Tarenam).
- **The Vethra** holds **1** (Kesveth — won in the Third Vethra War, KE 555–560).
- **The Phre enclaves** hold **1** (Voren — in the enclave zone).

So the matha's political problem is: **its apex (the Great Temple) is inside the weakest polity (the rump), while its 7 Head Temples are distributed across all five polities, including a foreign one (the Phre).** The matha's unity (REL-02 §4 "the one institution that survives all five eras intact") is now a **cross-polity institution** — its politics is the politics of keeping a single sacred institution coherent across five rival polities.

**Which houses controlled them?** (The DYN-03 houses, post-fragmentation status — see §17.)
- The **Gheshar house** (the founding Shreshtha's line, DYN-03 §1) rules the rump through the **Shreshtha office** (which it dominates) + the new **Chiefs' Council** (§13).
- The **Threnath house** (the founding garrison-commander's line, DYN-03 §1) rules the Sareth Dhaneshra through the **Sareth Dhresh** office.
- The **Keshath house** (the Tarn's leading house, the founding-marriage descendant, DYN-03 §2 #1/§3) leads the Tarn Confederation (with the Taren and Orenam houses).
- The **Veth royal house** (the Vethan line, FOR-02 §2) rules the Vethra (Vethan II's successor, §17).
- The **Phrean/Shoran families** (the alternating family tenure, TIM-03 §8/Q-073) govern the Phre enclaves (the Shoran family from post-KE 680, §17).
- The **Veshna matha** (the apex at the Great Temple + the 7 Head Temple heads) is the cross-polity sacred institution.

**Which rulers survived / disappeared?**
- **Survived into the Dhaneshra Period:** the Gheshar house's Shreshtha (the rump's head); the Threnath house's Sareth Dhresh (the Sareth's head); the Keshath house's head (the Tarn's leading house); the Veth royal house (Vethan II's line); the Phrean/Shoran families (the Phre's governors); the 4 Khor clans (the Kheshur/Ghoranur/Zhanur/Marenur, the seasonal assembly).
- **Disappeared:** the **House of Kesra** (deposed KE 675) — its line survives only as the **Temple Line** (sheltered at the Great Temple, §5). The imperial **King** (the consecrated protector-of-dharma) is gone — the **Empty Throne** (§8).

**What institutions survived / fractured?**
- **Survived:** the Veshna matha (the apex + the 7 Head Temples + the local shrines); the temple lands (~10–15% of the arable, tax-exempt, REL-02 §4); the consecration rite (the rite is intact, REL-02 §3); the Veshna sacred script (the frozen orthography, CUL-02 §2); the Shastras training (the priestly self-reproduction, REL-02 §4); the 4-law legal pluralism (the king's law → now the ruler's law; the jati law; the temple law; the port law, ADM-02 §2); the Shreshtha/Beshara/Dhresh offices (in the rump); the Tarnesh fourth (the Tarn's internal arrangement); the Khor clans + the seasonal assembly; the Phre chartered company (the family tenure); the Veth faith (a Veshna variant, REL-02 §6); the Phre faith (the Vorenavi, REL-02 §6).
- **Fractured:** the matha's **political unity** (the apex's authority over the 7 Head Temples is broken — they are now in 5 polities); the matha's **internal split** (the Tarnesh Dawn's Tarn wing vs. the Veshna orthodoxy wing, REL-02 §5) is now **geographically cemented** (the Tarn wing's seat = Tarenam, in the Tarn Confederation; the Veshna wing's seat = the Great Temple, in the rump); the **consecration rite's object** (there is no king to consecrate — the Empty Throne); the **unified protector-of-dharma office** (now five rulers, each a claimant); the **unified Beshara administration** (now five polities' administrations).

**What claims to legitimacy existed?** (§7) The four claims to the Kesra legacy: the **Temple Line** (the deposed House of Kesra, the true line); the **Keshath house** (the founding-marriage descendant, DYN-03 §2 #1); the **Gheshar house** (the founding Shreshtha's line, the office claim); the **Threnath house** (the defender claim, the Sareth's garrison command). Plus the **Vethra's Veshna-variant faith claim** (the Veth faith as a Veshna variant, REL-02 §6 — the Vethra claims to be a Veshna polity, not a foreign one) and the **Phre's external/charter claim** (no Veshna claim; the charter + trade + paramountcy).

**What competing succession claims existed?** (§7) The four claims to the **Empty Throne** (the protector-of-dharma title): the Temple Line (the true line, sheltered at the Great Temple); the Keshath (the founding-marriage descendant); the Gheshar (the founding Shreshtha's line, the office); the Threnath (the defender). The matha's dilemma: **consecrating one claimant means allying with one polity against the others** (the Temple Line/Gheshar = the rump; the Keshath = the Tarn; the Threnath = the Sareth).

**What territories / resources were controlled / contested?** (§9) The rump: the bār core (the fiscal heartland; the Oren canal; the 7 Grand Districts; Besra the grain market) + the Khor frontier (Keshkhor; the horse trade) + the Great Temple (the apex) + 3 Head Temples. The Sareth: the passes (the tolls) + the iron/timber (the Sareth foothills/forests, GEO-01 §5) + the Sareth mine (the silver, GEO-01 §5). The Tarn: the paddy plain (the high yield; the Tarnesh fourth) + the Oren crossings (the tolls) + Tarenam. The Vethra: the Veth south + the southern fringe (won KE 555–560) + the pepper (the Vetham monopoly) + the Veth coast + Kesveth. The Phre: the Voren enclaves (the delta mouth, the chokepoint) + the sea-lanes + the Phre silver (a lifeline + a leash, ECO-02 §6). **Contested:** the Oren (the spine — all polities depend on it; navigation rights/tolls); the delta mouth/Voren (the rump's claim vs. the Phre enclaves); Keshkhor/the horse trade (the rump + the Khor); the pepper (the Vethra monopoly vs. the rump's obsolete state-monopoly claim); the temple lands (tax-exempt, in 5 polities — each polity wants to control its own); the Great Temple/Veshna (the rump's biggest legitimacy asset; the other polities want access/influence).

**What alliances formed / rivalries emerged?** (§11) The rump↔Tarn rivalry (the Keshath dynastic claim vs. the rump's institutional + Temple Line claim) + cooperation (the Oren heartland + crossings); the rump↔Sareth trade-dependence (the iron/timber) + legitimacy-rivalry (the Threnath defender claim vs. the rump's Kesra legacy); the Vethra↔Phre trade-partnership (the pepper) + competition (the delta); the Sareth as the balancing power (the iron/timber + the dual silver); the Khor as the rump's strategic dependency (the horses + the silver).

**What external powers influenced matha politics?** (§12) The **Phre** (the silver leash + the delta mouth + the sea-lanes + the Voren Head Temple in its enclave zone); the **Khor** (the rump's horse/silver dependency); the **Vethra** (the Veshna-variant faith claim + the southern fringe).

**What internal factions existed?** (§10) Within the matha: the **Veshna apex wing** (the orthodoxy; the Great Temple; the consecration authority; the Temple Line's shelter); the **Tarn renewal wing** (the Tarnesh Dawn's heirs; Tarenam; the Tarn vernacular); the **conciliar faction** (the argument for the Sabha of the 7 Head Temple heads vs. the Veshna apex hierarchy); the **pragmatist faction** (the argument for the separate Concordats with each polity); the **dynastic-patronage faction** (the argument to consecrate a new king, to restore the protector-of-dharma order); the **apex-neutrality faction** (the argument that the matha must not pick a side, or it becomes a tool of one polity).

**What political compromises became necessary?** (§13) The **Consecration Moratorium** (the matha will not consecrate a new king while the claims are contested — the apex neutrality); the **Defenders' Blessing** (a new lesser rite for non-consecrated rulers — the institutional evolution); the **five Concordats** (the separate matha-polity agreements on the temple lands' tax-exemption + autonomy); the **Veshna Sabha** (the conciliar body of the apex + the 7 Head Temple heads); the **Defenders' Council** (the polities' diplomatic mechanism for the Oren/iron-timber trade); the rump's **Chiefs' Council** (the post-dynastic government).

**What consequences followed?** (§15) The **Empty Throne** (the protector-of-dharma title is empty; the polities rule as blessed Defenders, not consecrated Kings); the matha's **two-wing + conciliar + concordat** political system; the **four claims** as the standing tension (the Empty Throne can be filled by any of them); the **Oren** as the polities' diplomatic center; the **Phre's paramountcy** grows (but the rump-Khor silver axis + the Sareth's dual silver limit it); the **Vethra** stays inside the Veshna fold (the Veth faith + the Kesveth Concordat); the **Khor** stays the rump's strategic axis. The Dhaneshra Period's equilibrium (post-KE ~900).

## 2. Initial post-fragmentation conditions (the Dhaneshra Period opens, post-KE 675)

The Dhaneshra Period opens at the **House of Kesra's deposition (KE 675)** — the rump's dynastic legitimacy ends; the rump is now ruled by the chiefs + the Shreshtha (the Gheshar house). The **five polities** are in place (the Tarn Confederation KE 595; the Sareth Dhaneshra KE 610; the Kesra Dhaneshra KE 650; the Vethra risen KE 550–620; the Phre enclaves KE 510/530). The **Veshna temple network** is geographically split across the five polities (§1). The **consecration rite** is intact but its object is gone (no king). The **protector-of-dharma title** is empty. The **matha's internal split** (the Tarn wing vs. the Veshna wing) is now geographically cemented. The **resources** are distributed (the rump's bār core + Khor; the Sareth's iron/timber + mine; the Tarn's paddy; the Vethra's pepper + coast; the Phre's delta mouth + silver + sea-lanes). The **Oren** is the common resource (all polities depend on it). The **population** is ~25M (the post-fragmentation contraction, DEM-02). The **army** is bounded by the 0.3% ceiling (MIL-02 §1); the **no-blue-water navy** (MIL-02 §3) means the Phre controls the sea-lanes.

## 3. Political actors (the Dhaneshra Period's cast)

- **The Kesra Dhaneshra (the rump):** the **Gheshar house's Shreshtha** (the rump's head, the office it dominates) + the **Chiefs' Council** (§13, the post-dynastic government) + the **Temple Line** (the deposed House of Kesra, sheltered at the Great Temple, §5). Holds the bār core + the Khor frontier + the Great Temple + 3 Head Temples.
- **The Sareth Dhaneshra:** the **Threnath house's Sareth Dhresh** (the Sareth's head) + the **Gheshar house** (the Gheshar waystation, DYN-03 §5). Holds the passes + the iron/timber + the Sareth mine.
- **The Tarn Confederation:** the **Keshath house's head** (the leading house, the founding-marriage descendant) + the **Taren house** + the **Orenam house** (the 3 Tarn houses, DYN-03 §3). Holds the paddy plain + the Oren crossings + Tarenam.
- **The Vethra:** the **Veth royal house** (Vethan II's successor, §17) + the **Veth faith** (a Veshna variant, REL-02 §6). Holds the Veth south + the southern fringe + the pepper + the Veth coast + Kesveth.
- **The Phre enclaves:** the **Phrean/Shoran families** (the Shoran family from post-KE 680, §17) + the **Phre faith** (the Vorenavi, REL-02 §6). Holds the Voren enclaves (the delta mouth) + the sea-lanes + the Phre silver.
- **The Veshna matha:** the **apex (the Veshna Great Temple's guru, at Veshna, in the rump)** + the **7 Regional Head Temple heads** (Kesra/Besra/Keshkhor in the rump; Threna in the Sareth; Tarenam in the Tarn; Kesveth in the Vethra; Voren in the Phre zone) + the two wings (the Veshna apex wing, the Tarn renewal wing, §10).
- **The Khor:** the **4 clans** (the Kheshur/Ghoranur/Zhanur/Marenur) + the **seasonal assembly** (the Kheshur's leader as the traditional senior, DYN-03 §6). The rump's strategic dependency (the horses + the silver).

## 5. The Temple Line (the deposed House of Kesra at the Great Temple)

The **deposed House of Kesra's line** (the true line, the consecrated royal house) takes refuge at the **Veshna Great Temple** (post-KE 675). **Causal basis:** the matha's **sacred obligation to protect kings** (the consecration rite, REL-02 §3 — the matha consecrates and shelters the king) + the matha's **deterrence function** (REL-02 §8 — the de-consecration is a deterrence that is never consummated; the matha's authority deters the chiefs from killing the consecrated line — killing a consecrated royal is a sacrilege the matha would condemn, and the matha's authority depends on the rite's sanctity). So the **Temple Line** survives at the Great Temple under the matha's protection, as a **political actor** (the true-line claim, §7). The rump's chiefs do not kill the line (the matha's deterrence) but do not restore it (the deposition is political/economic, not religious — REL-02 §8). The **Temple Line's presence at the Great Temple** is the rump's biggest legitimacy asset (the true line is in the rump, at the apex) — and the matha's biggest dilemma (it shelters the true line, which is the rump's claim, while the Tarn's Keshath claim and the Sareth's Threnath claim compete).

## 6. The matha's two wings (the split, geographically cemented)

The matha's internal split (the Tarnesh Dawn, REL-02 §5) is now **geographically cemented** by the fragmentation:
- **The Veshna apex wing** (the orthodoxy; the **Veshna Great Temple** at Veshna, in the rump; the **guru's seat**; the consecration authority; the Veshna sacred script; the Shastras training; the **Temple Line's shelter**). The apex.
- **The Tarn renewal wing** (the Tarnesh Dawn's heirs; the **Tarenam Head Temple**, in the Tarn Confederation; the Tarn vernacular; the Tarn's religious glue, REL-02 §4). The Tarn wing's seat is now a Regional Head Temple in the Tarn Confederation — the Tarn wing is the Tarn's religious house.

The two wings' **seats are in different polities** (the Veshna wing in the rump; the Tarn wing in the Tarn) — the matha's internal split is now a **cross-polity split**. The matha's unity (REL-02 §4) is now a **political problem** (keeping a single sacred institution coherent across two wings in two polities, + the 7 Head Temples in five polities).

## 7. The four claims to the Kesra legacy (the Empty Throne's claimants)

**The matha's dilemma:** consecrating one claimant means **allying with one polity against the others** (the Temple Line/Gheshar = the rump; the Keshath = the Tarn; the Threnath = the Sareth). This is the **Empty Throne** (§8).

## 8. The Empty Throne (the consecration moratorium)

The **protector-of-dharma title** (the king's title, REL-02 §3) is **empty** — there is no consecrated king (the House of Kesra is deposed; the claims are contested). The matha's **Consecration Moratorium** (the apex neutrality decision, §13): the matha will not consecrate a new king while the claims are contested. The **consecration rite** survives (the rite is intact, REL-02 §3) but its **object is frozen** (no king to consecrate). The **Empty Throne** is the Dhaneshra Period's defining feature: the polities rule as **blessed Defenders** (the Defenders' Blessing, §13), not **consecrated Kings**. The **four claims** (§7) are the standing tension: the Empty Throne can be filled by any of them — the succession question is the Dhaneshra Period's open political question (→ Q-080).

## 9. Territorial claims + contested resources (the polities' territorial politics)

- **The Oren** — the spine (GEO-01 §2); all polities depend on it (the rump's canal; the Tarn's crossings; the Sareth's export route; the Vethra's delta exit; the Phre's delta mouth). **Contested:** the navigation rights + the tolls + the delta exit. The Oren is the polities' **diplomatic center** (the Defenders' Council, §13).
- **The delta mouth/Voren** — the chokepoint (GEO-01 §2); the **rump's claim** (Voren is a Kesra city, the Vorenesh office, ADM-02 §1) vs. the **Phre enclaves** (the First Enclave KE 510, the Second Enclave KE 530, TIM-03 §5/§6). **Contested:** the delta mouth (the rump's claim vs. the Phre's enclaves).
- **Keshkhor/the horse trade** — the rump + the Khor (the rump's Khor frontier, the Keshkhor district, DYN-03 §5); the Sareth's interest (the Sareth needs the horses; its access depends on the rump or the Khor). **Contested:** the horse trade (the rump's monopoly claim vs. the Sareth's access).
- **The pepper** — the Vethra monopoly (the Vetham, ECO-02 §5); the rump's obsolete state-monopoly claim (the old Samur pepper monopoly via Kesveth, ECO-02 §2 — now Kesveth is the Vethra's). **Contested:** the pepper (the Vethra monopoly vs. the rump's obsolete claim).
- **The temple lands** — tax-exempt, ~10–15% of the arable (REL-02 §4), now in 5 polities. **Contested:** each polity wants to control the temple lands in its territory; the matha wants the autonomy (the Concordats, §13).
- **The Great Temple/Veshna** — the apex (the rump's biggest legitimacy asset); the other polities want access/influence. **Contested:** the Great Temple (the rump's asset vs. the other polities' claim).
- **The iron/timber** — the Sareth foothills/forests (GEO-01 §5); all polities need it. **Contested:** the iron/timber trade (the Sareth's leverage).
- **The silver** — the Khor highlands + the Sareth mine (GEO-01 §5) + the Phre's island silver (ECO-02 §6). **Contested:** the silver (the Phre's leash vs. the rump-Khor + Sareth's counter-leverage).

## 10. The matha's internal factions (the post-fragmentation matha politics)

- **The Veshna apex wing** (the orthodoxy; the Great Temple; the consecration authority; the Temple Line's shelter) — the apex; the claim: the Veshna apex is the matha's true seat (the guru's seat, REL-02 §4).
- **The Tarn renewal wing** (the Tarnesh Dawn's heirs; Tarenam; the Tarn vernacular) — the Tarn's religious house; the claim: the Tarn wing is the matha's renewal (the Tarnesh Dawn, REL-02 §5); the Tarenam Head Temple is the matha's Tarn seat.
- **The conciliar faction** (the argument for the **Veshna Sabha** — the council of the apex + the 7 Head Temple heads — vs. the Veshna apex hierarchy) — the claim: the geographic fragmentation (the 7 Head Temples in 5 polities) forces a conciliar body (the Sabha) to keep the matha coherent.
- **The pragmatist faction** (the argument for the **five Concordats** — the separate matha-polity agreements) — the claim: the temple lands' autonomy (in 5 polities) requires separate agreements with each polity (the Concordats).
- **The dynastic-patronage faction** (the argument to **consecrate a new king** — to restore the protector-of-dharma order) — the claim: the Empty Throne (the consecration moratorium) is a failure (the matha should consecrate a king, to restore the protector-of-dharma order). (This faction is split by the four claims — the Veshna wing's dynastic-patronage faction backs the Temple Line/Gheshar; the Tarn wing's dynastic-patronage faction backs the Keshath.)
- **The apex-neutrality faction** (the argument that the matha must **not pick a side** — or it becomes a tool of one polity) — the claim: the matha's authority depends on its neutrality (the Consecration Moratorium, the Empty Throne).

## 11. Alliances + rivalries (the polities' relationships)

- **The rump ↔ the Tarn:** the **rivalry** (the Keshath dynastic claim vs. the rump's institutional + Temple Line claim — the two strongest "Kesra legacy" claims compete) + the **cooperation** (the Oren heartland + crossings — the rump + the Tarn together hold the Oren). The rump-Tarn relationship is the Dhaneshra Period's central Veshna rivalry.
- **The rump ↔ the Sareth:** the **trade-dependence** (the rump needs the Sareth's iron/timber for the canal/military; the Sareth needs the rump's Oren route for its exports) + the **legitimacy-rivalry** (the Threnath defender claim vs. the rump's Kesra legacy). The rump-Sareth relationship is a trade-dependent relationship with a legitimacy rivalry.
- **The Vethra ↔ the Phre:** the **trade-partnership** (the Vethra's pepper is the Phre's main export; the Phre's delta mouth is the Vethra's exit) + the **competition** (the delta; the Phre-Vethra delta competition, TIM-03 §6). The Vethra is squeezed (the Phre's leash + the Oren).
- **The Sareth as the balancing power:** the Sareth's iron/timber + the dual silver (the Sareth mine) make it the key balancing power (every polity needs it; it can tip the balance).
- **The Khor as the rump's strategic dependency:** the rump's Khor frontier (the Keshkhor; the horse/silver trade) is the rump's strategic axis (the horses + the silver); the Khor clans play the polities against each other (the horse/silver trade).

## 13. The institutional mechanisms (the Dhaneshra Period's political system)

- **The Veshna Sabha** (the conciliar body, post-KE ~700): the **council of the apex (the Veshna Great Temple's guru) + the 7 Regional Head Temple heads** = **8 seats** (3 in the rump [Kesra/Besra/Keshkhor], 1 in the Sareth [Threna], 1 in the Tarn [Tarenam], 1 in the Vethra [Kesveth], 1 in the Phre zone [Voren]). - **The five Concordats** (the pragmatist faction's compromise, post-KE ~700–750): the **separate matha-polity agreements** on the temple lands' tax-exemption + autonomy — one per polity:
  - **The Veshna/Kesra/Besra/Keshkhor Concordat** (the rump): the temple lands' tax-exemption + autonomy in the rump; the Great Temple's apex status; the rump's non-interference in the matha's internal affairs; the matha's blessing of the rump's Shreshtha (the Defenders' Blessing).

  - **The Voren Concordat** (the Phre zone): the Voren Head Temple's autonomy (in the Phre enclave zone); the Phre's non-interference; the port law's temple-law clause (the ADM-02 §2 port law); the matha's recognition of the Phre's delta trade access.
