# Transformation Log — DEM-01 (Demography)

Date: 2026-08-26
Canon target: **DEM-01** (the ~25–40 million population; the dense-core/thin-border geometry; the wind-dependent harvest; the river/sea epidemic; the cyclical labor supply)
Method: the 5-step sequential reasoning (demography as material reasoning calibrated to the models).

## Step 1 — Historical examples and underlying mechanisms

- **Mughal + Qing** — the **population-scale calibration** (a great-river agrarian empire of tens of millions; the dense core + the thin frontier).
- **All models** — the **famine → debt → revolt chain** (the wind-dependent harvest fails → the debt grows → the revolt when the tax is still demanded).
- **All models** — the **epidemic** (the dense core + the river/sea highway → the disease travels the same routes as the trade).

## Step 2 — Original historical conditions

- The Mughal/Qing scale (the great-river plain, the dense core, the thin frontier).
- The wind-dependent harvest (the paddy, the monsoon).
- The dense core + the river/sea highway (the epidemic vector).

## Step 3 — Equivalent or contrasting Samur conditions

- The Samur population is **~25–40 million**, **concentrated in the bār core + the pell coast** (~70–80% on ~50% of the land), the **borders thin** (a small, expensive garrison).
- The **seasonal wind** (GEO-01 §7) is the **harvest driver** → the **famine → debt → revolt chain**.
- The **Oren highway + the Voren port** → the **epidemic** travels the river and the sea.
- **Contrast (the load-bearing difference):** the Samur's **labor supply is concentrated in the wind-dependent agrarian core** (the bār + the pell), so the **fiscal-military system is directly vulnerable to the wind** — and, because of the **three-front overstretch** (GEO-01), the **small, expensive garrison** is *more* vulnerable to a labor-supply shock than in the models, where the frontier was (relatively) less overextended.

## Step 4 — Original Samur solution

The **demographic structure** (~25–40 million; the dense core + the thin border; the wind-dependent harvest; the river/sea epidemic; the cyclical labor supply) is the **Samur-specific** demographic base — calibrated to the Mughal/Qing scale but shaped by the **Samur geography** (the Oren, the wind, the coast, the three fronts). It is **not a reskin**: the **interaction of the wind-dependent labor supply with the three-front overstretch** is the load-bearing difference.

## Step 5 — Consequences

- **Immediate:** the population ~25–40 million; the concentration in the core + the coast; the thin border; the cyclical labor supply.
- **Unintended:** the **fiscal-military geometry** (the small garrison + the large, dense, taxable core) → the vulnerability at the border *and* the vulnerability at the core; the **famine → debt → revolt chain** as the standing social fault line.
- **Long-term:** the **demographic cycle** (the good-wind growth, the failed-wind / epidemic contraction) is a **root cause** of the Phase 5 decline, interacting with the **fiscal-military overstretch** and the **elite competition**.
