> ECO-01 is the economy as a **structure** (agriculture, currency, trade, monopoly, the merchant/banker, the craft/industrialization, the court as engine). The *specific* prices, quantities, and trade volumes are the later ECO files.

## 1. The agricultural base

- **The bār** (the dry-summer cereal, the core) — the **tax grain**, the fiscal base. Irrigated (the tanks/anicut + the Oren canals, ADM-01 §6). The state directs the major works. The bār is the **stable** base (the dry-summer cereal is less wind-dependent than the paddy).
- **The pell** (the wet paddy, the coast/delta/Tarn) — the **denser** zone, the **export** zone (the rice), but **wind-dependent** (the famine risk, DEM-01). The pell is the **volatile** base (the good-wind boom, the failed-wind famine).
- **The cash crops** (the indigo, the cotton, the sugarcane) — the **monetization** (the push for the silver cash) forces the peasant to grow the **cash crops** (to sell for the silver) → the **moneylender** (the shahukar) → the **peasant debt** (the Mughal logic). The cash crops are the **social fault line** (the revolt when the targets inflate, TIM-01).
- **The state monopoly on the farm products** — the **salt** (essential), the **pepper** (the high-value coastal good).

## 2. The currency (the silver standard)

- **The silver coin** is the currency, **minted by the state** (the mints at Kesra, Besra, Voren, Threna, Keshkhor). The **silver standard** — the coin's value is its silver content.
- **The dual currency** — the silver coin (the cash) + the grain (the tax-in-kind, the bār). The **monetization** is the shift from the grain-in-kind to the silver-cash (the Mughal zabt cash-revenue logic).
- **The silver supply** — from the **frontier** (the Khor highlands + the Sareth mines, GEO-01 §5). The silver enters via the **frontier trade** (the Khor horse trade brings the silver; the Sareth trade brings the silver). So the **monetization is tied to the frontier** — a **failed frontier** (the Khor, the Sareth) is a **silver shortage** = a **monetary crisis** (the tax crisis, ADM-01 §4).
- **The moneylender (the shahukar)** — the merchant-banker who advances the **silver** to the peasant (to pay the tax) and collects **with interest** → the **peasant debt** (the monetization chain, the social fault line).

## 3. The trade system

- **The Oren** — the **internal trade artery** (the grain moves down the Oren; the goods move up and down the Oren). The Oren is the **logistics spine** (GEO-01 §2).
- **The Sareth passes** — the **western trade** (the iron/timber in, the bār/textiles out). The passes are the **chokepoints** (the transit/market dues).
- **The Khor horse trade** — the **northern trade** (the horses in, the silver/salt/bār out). The **horse trade is a strategic lifeline** (the cavalry depends on the Khor horses, GEO-01 §5).
- **The delta/pepper (Voren)** — the **maritime trade** (the pepper, the textiles, the salt out; the silver, the foreign goods in). **Voren is the maritime hub** (GEO-02). The **delta mouth** is the chokepoint (the reverse-invasion axis, GEO-01 §2).
- **The Veth coast (Kesveth)** — the **pepper coast** (the Vethra rivalry, GEO-01 §8.5).
- **The long-distance trade** — the Samur **cloth** to the wider world (the Maratha cloth-export logic), the **pepper** to the maritime partners. The **trade wealth** is at the coast (the delta, the Veth).

## 4. The state monopoly (the state's commercial power)

- **Monopolized** (the state's direct control): the **salt** (essential), the **horses** (strategic, the Khor — the state controls the horse supply → controls the cavalry), the **pepper** (the high-value coastal good, the most contested).
- **Taxed** (not monopolized): the **textiles** (the bulk, the craft guilds), the **transit/market dues** (the passes, the port, the market).
- **The state as the largest purchaser/employer** (the Mughal logic) — the court, the garrisons, and the workshops **buy and employ** (the state demand is the market anchor).

## 5. The merchant/banker class (the economic actor)

- **The shahukar (the merchant-banker)** — **finances the war** (the debt-financed war, the Maratha logic), **controls the monetization** (the debt), **trades** (the cloth, the pepper, the silver). The **urban/commercial power** (Besra, Voren).
- **The growth imperative** — the **debt-financed war must keep conquering** (the captured revenue repays the loan) → the empire is **structurally pulled toward expansion** (DYN-01 §5, TIM-01). This is a **root cause** of the overstretch.
- **The merchant/banker vs. the state** — the merchant/banker **resists** the state's attempts to control the cash (the monopoly, the tax) — the **revenue contest** (ADM-01 §7).

## 6. The craft guilds and the state workshops (the industrialization)

- **The craft guilds** — the **silk**, the **steel**, the **textiles**, the **paper** (the Mysore logic) — the **urban producers** (Besra, Voren, Kesra).
- **The state workshops (the karkhanas)** — the **state-directed manufacturing** (the Mysore logic) — the **military demand** (the steel, the guns for the frontier) + the **court demand** (the silk, the textiles) drive the **metallurgy and the crafts** — a **dual-demand industrialization**.
- **The court as the economic engine** (the Mysore logic) — the court at Kesra + the temple at Veshna are the **market anchors** (the state demand). The court **consumes and displays** → the **high-value crafts** (the silk, the steel, the textiles) are produced **for the court**. The court's **taste sets the production standard**.

## 7. Who benefits / who resists (the economic level)

**Benefits:** the **state** (the revenue, the monopoly), the **Shreshtha** (the power), the **court** (the patronage, the market anchor), the **merchant/bankers** (the trade, the debt, the war finance), the **craft guilds** (the court demand), the **port** (the trade wealth).

**Resists:** the **chiefs** (the tax evasion, the keeping the surplus), the **peasants** (the inflated targets, the cash-crop coercion, the moneylender debt), the **temples** (the tax-exempt land, the parallel economy), the **port** (the contested transit taxes, the Phre), the **frontier** (the silver supply).

**The structural tension:** the **economy is a contest** — the **state** (the Shreshtha, the Revenue Head) wants to **maximize the revenue** (the monopoly, the tax, the monetization), but the **chiefs** (the evasion), the **peasants** (the revolt), the **temples** (the parallel economy), the **port** (the contested trade), and the **frontier** (the silver supply) all **constrain** it — so the **economic engine is always under strain** (the fiscal-military tension, DEM-01, TIM-01).

## 8. The economic consequences (tied to the decline)

- **The monetization chain** (the silver → the cash → the cash crop → the moneylender → the debt → the revolt) is the **social fault line** (DEM-01, TIM-01).
- **The growth imperative** (the debt-financed war) is a **root cause** of the overstretch (TIM-01).
- **The silver supply** (the frontier) ties the **economy to the frontier** (a failed frontier = a monetary crisis).
- **The court as market anchor** (the Mysore) — the **economic center of gravity** (the court, the temple).
- **The Phre** (the delta) — **captures the maritime trade** (Voren) → the **economic center of gravity shifts** (GEO-02, TIM-01).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the agricultural base (the bār, the pell, the cash crops); the currency (the silver standard, the dual currency, the monetary crisis); the trade system (the Oren, the passes, the Khor, the delta, the Veth, the long-distance); the state monopoly (the salt, the horses, the pepper); the merchant/banker (the war finance, the growth imperative); the craft/industrialization (the guilds, the workshops, the court as engine); who benefits/who resists.
- **Unintended:** the monetization chain is the social fault line (the revolt); the growth imperative is a root cause of the overstretch; the silver supply ties the economy to the frontier (a failed frontier = a monetary crisis); the court as market anchor (the economic center of gravity); the Phre captures the maritime trade (the center of gravity shifts).
- **Long-term:** the economic contest (the state vs. the five constrainers) is a root cause of the fiscal stress (TIM-01); the monetization chain is the social fault line (DEM-01); the growth imperative is the overstretch (TIM-01); the Phre's capture of Voren is the **economic trigger** of the fragmentation (TIM-01).
