## 1. The toponymy (the fixed register)

The **Samur language** (the core/administrative language) is the language of the toponyms. The **toponymic register** (the phonology):

- **Consonants:** k, kh, g, gh, t, th, d, dh, p, ph, b, bh, ṣ, s, sh, zh, r, l, m, n, ṇ, v, y, h, z.
- **Vowels:** a, ā, i, ī, u, ū, e, ai, o, au.
- A **consonant-rich** register (a plausible medieval agrarian-imperial language).

**The place names (now fixed):**

- **The continent:** **Veshran**.
- **The rivers:** the **Oren** (the great river), the **Khel** (the west tributary), the **Tarn** (the east tributary/plain).
- **The borders:** the **Sareth** (the western wall), the **Khor** (the northern steppe), the **Veth** shore (the southern coast).
- **The cities:** **Kesra** (the capital), **Veshna** (the temple city), **Besra** (the grain market), **Threna** (the western fortress), **Gheshar** (the waystation), **Keshkhor** (the horse emporium), **Voren** (the delta port), **Kesveth** (the Veth port).
- **The states:** **Vethra** (the southern state); **the Phre** (the maritime power — a Samur rendering of the Phre's own name, a loanword).
- **The passes:** the **Thren Gate** (the main), the **Vesh Gate** (the second), the **Ghul Gate** (the third).
- **The people:** the **Samur** (the founding people).
- **The grains:** **bār** (the dry-summer cereal), **pell** (the wet paddy).
- **The era:** the **Kesra Era** (KE).
- **The offices:** the **Shreshtha** (the prime minister/regent), the **Veshna matha** (the temple/priestly council).

All the toponyms are in the **Samur language** (the core register), except **Phre** (a Samur rendering of the maritime power's name, a loanword).

## 2. The languages and dialects (resolves Q-007)

The empire is a **multi-lingual empire** with a **dominant administrative language**:

1. **The Samur language** — the **core/administrative** language (the heartland, the court, the bureaucracy, the Veshna commentarial tradition). The **dominant** language. The toponyms are in this language.
2. **The coastal trade pidgin** (the **delta trade tongue**) — the **coastal/delta** language (the port, the maritime trade, Voren, the Phre). A **trade pidgin** (the lingua franca of the delta/Voren).
3. **The Khor language** — the **steppe** language (the horse-people, the north). The **Khor faith** is in this language.
4. **The Tarn language(s)** — the **tributary east** language(s) (the local, the Tarn Marches). Possibly **multiple** languages.
5. **The Veth language** — the **southern** language (the Vethra, the "state too strong"). The **Veth faith** is in this language.
6. **The Phre language** — the **maritime** language (the Phre, the chartered company, the delta). The **port faith** is in this language.

**The dialects** (the regional varieties of the Samur language):

- The **core dialect** (the heartland, the upper Oren) — the **standard** (the court, the Veshna).
- The **middle-Oren dialect** (the Middle Oren, Besra) — the **grain/mercantile** dialect.
- The **Sareth dialect** (the western wall, Threna) — the **frontier/military** dialect.
- The **delta dialect** (the delta, Voren) — the **maritime/trade** dialect (mixed with the coastal pidgin).

**Q-007 is resolved:** the empire is a **multi-lingual empire** with a **dominant administrative language** (the Samur language) — the core Samur language + the coastal trade pidgin + the Khor + the Tarn + the Veth + the Phre.

## 3. The literature

- **The Veshna commentarial tradition** — the **sacred/literary** literature (the Veshna faith's texts, the commentaries, the dharma grammar). The **high literature** (the priestly class, the Veshna matha). In the **Samur language** (the classical/standard).
- **The vernacular revival literature** (the Q-040) — the **popular devotional** literature (the vernacular, the direct, the anti-hierarchical). The **vernacular** (the regional dialects). The **bhakti** logic (the Sanatan revival, REL-01 §4). This creates the **vernacular literary culture**.
- **The court/chronicle literature** — the **royal chronicles** (the founding myth, the kings, the wars). The **court** (the Shreshtha, the king). In the **Samur language** (the standard).
- **The mercantile/trade literature** — the **trade records, the port documents** (the Oren, the Voren, the trade). The **merchant/banker** (the shahukar). In the **coastal pidgin / the Samur language**.
- **The Khor oral tradition** — the **steppe** oral literature (the horse-people, the Khor faith). The **Khor language** (oral, not written).

The literature is **stratified** (the high [the Veshna commentarial, the court chronicles] + the vernacular [the revival] + the mercantile [the trade] + the oral [the Khor]). This is the **literary culture** (the REL-01 §4 orthodoxy vs. revival + the Q-040).

## 4. The everyday life (the texture of the five factions + the minorities)

The everyday life is the **texture of the five factions + the minorities** (the peasants, the merchants, the chiefs, the military, the court, the minorities).

## 5. The historical memory (how the empire imperfectly understands its own past)

The empire's **self-understanding** of its past is **imperfect** — the **founding myth**, the **Veshna consecration legend**, the **distortions** that accumulate over the eras:

- **The founding myth** — the **Kesra I** legend (the unification, the chokepoint, the temple) — the **official** founding narrative (the court, the Veshna). But it is **idealized** (the myth) — the **real** founding (the material, the absorption of the chiefs) is **distorted** (the myth emphasizes the legitimacy, not the material conquest).
- **The Veshna consecration legend** — the **sacred** founding narrative (the temple sanctions the king, the consecration) — the **religious** founding (the matha). This **elevates** the matha (the sanctioner) and **distorts** the **real** founding (the dual structure, the Shreshtha's role).
- **The distortions** (the accumulation over the eras):
  - The **High Empire** (the great building era, the Veshna great temple) — the **glorification** of the High Empire (the "golden age" myth).
  - The **Stress** (the famine, the epidemic, the revolt) — the **distortion** of the Stress (the "decline" narrative, the blame on the failures, the wind, the king).
  - The **Fragmentation** (the successor states, the Phre) — the **distortion** of the Fragmentation (the "loss" narrative, the blame on the Phre, the Vethra, the succession).
- **The imperfect memory** — the empire (and its successor states) **imperfectly understand** their own past — the **myths** (the founding, the consecration, the golden age), the **distortions** (the Stress, the Fragmentation), the **losses** (the forgotten, the erased, the silenced).

This is the **historical memory** (the Phase 4 requirement) — the **imperfect self-understanding** of the past (the myths, the distortions, the losses).
