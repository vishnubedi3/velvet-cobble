# Taxonomy 16 — Romance & Interpersonal Relationship Tells

**Sources for this cluster:** S03, S06, S28, S29, S37, S42.
**Dominant causes:** K3 (explicitness, fast resolution), K4 (romance templates), K1 (generic intimacy language), K7 (sanitized friction).

> Framing note (per the research brief): these are tells of *interpersonal
> storytelling*, not genre put-downs. Romance's conventions (HEA, emotional
> transparency beats) are legitimate contracts; the tells are the places where
> generated text over-executes them or applies them where the author didn't.

---

## R01 — Instant emotional transparency in attraction

- **Definition.** Attraction and its causes are made explicit immediately —
  characters know and can articulate why they feel what they feel, on schedule.
- **Example pattern.** First meeting → immediate internal cataloguing of the
  other's attractive qualities → the narrator states the attraction outright.
- **Observable characteristics.** Attraction labeled at first contact; CAspER:
  romance characters are "nearly completely coherent, whole, transparent" —
  "straight-forward and not confused with emotions" (S29).
- **Evidence.** S29 (Tier 0, direct), S28 (Tier 0 explicitness). Confidence:
  **Medium**.
- **Likely cause.** K3: emotional clarity preference; K4: romance templates open
  with attraction.
- **Variation.** Genre: romance strongest (partly contract); most tell-like in
  literary/litfic relationships. Perspective: strongest in close POV.
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 2 — instant-attraction is a real
  genre beat; the tell is the *articulated* version (the character who can
  already explain it).
- **Effect on quality.** The relationship's mystery is spent at hello; the
  slow discovery of another person — the genre's engine — is skipped.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 2–3: keep the attraction, remove the
  *articulation*; let it surface as attention (what they notice, what they
  don't say) before it's named.
- **Side effects.** Frustrating opacity where the genre wants clarity.
- **Validation.** Genre-contract check (frameworks/07) + reader inference test.

## R02 — Milestone-checklist progression

- **Definition.** The relationship advances through a fixed milestone ladder —
  meet, misunderstand, resolve, confess, couple — each stage complete before the
  next begins, with no sideways motion.
- **Example pattern.** The beats arrive on the genre's canonical schedule; the
  relationship *state* changes only at milestone boundaries.
- **Observable characteristics.** Stage-ladder regularity; no plateaus,
  regressions, or parallel tracks; CAspER's transparent romance characters (S29);
  S03's formulaic structure.
- **Evidence.** S29 (Tier 0), S03 (Tier 0). Confidence: **Medium**.
- **Likely cause.** K4: romance's most probable plot is its canonical ladder;
  K2 can't deviate.
- **Variation.** Genre: romance strongest (contract); the tell is over-execution
  even there (the ladder with nothing on the rungs).
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 2.
- **Effect on quality.** The relationship is a progress bar; no texture between
  milestones.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 4: insert one off-ladder beat (a plateau, a
  backward step, a parallel relationship thread) from the story model's
  relationship register.
- **Side effects.** Breaking the genre's promise to readers (HEA pacing is
  contract — vary the rungs, not the ladder).
- **Validation.** Beat-ladder audit vs. genre norms.

## R03 — Residue-free reconciliation

- **Definition.** Conflicts between characters resolve completely: apologies are
  accepted, trust is restored, nothing is left over — the wound closes without
  a scar.
- **Example pattern.** The big fight ends in mutual understanding and the
  relationship returns to baseline (or better) immediately.
- **Observable characteristics.** Post-reconciliation state = pre-conflict state;
  no lingering mistrust, jokes, or hesitations; F04/T05's relationship face.
- **Evidence.** S03 (Tier 0: uniformly positive, weak turning points), K3
  mechanism. Confidence: **Medium**.
- **Likely cause.** K3: resolution preference; unresolved interpersonal states
  read as "incomplete" to the model.
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in romance/drama; weakest in literary.
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 2.
- **Effect on quality.** Relationships without history; every fight is
  resettable, so fights don't matter.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 3: leave one residue — the joke that lands
  wrong afterward, the hesitation, the thing that is forgiven but not forgotten.
- **Side effects.** Melodramatic grudge-holding.
- **Validation.** State-comparison audit: is post ≠ pre in at least one way?

## R04 — Over-explained internal feelings

- **Definition.** The interior experience of the relationship is narrated as
  analysis: feelings named, causes mapped, significance stated — the romance
  of the spreadsheet.
- **Example pattern.** "She loved him, she realized — not the way she'd loved
  Marcus, but something quieter, built from shared mornings and small kindnesses."
  (complete, correct, and dead).
- **Observable characteristics.** Emotion-analysis density in relationship
  scenes; E01/E02's romance face; S28's explicitness.
- **Evidence.** S28 (Tier 0), S37 (Tier 1). Confidence: **Medium-High**.
- **Likely cause.** K3 + K4: romance reviews and summaries train
  feeling-plus-interpretation as the register.
- **Variation.** Genre: romance/litfic strongest; weakest in hard-boiled.
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 2.
- **Effect on quality.** The reader is briefed on the relationship instead of
  living in it; interiority becomes captioning.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 2–3: keep one layer of feeling (the
  *specific* one — the shared mornings, not the taxonomy of love); cut the
  analysis.
- **Side effects.** Emotional flatness if the concrete layer isn't vivid.
- **Validation.** Concrete-detail test: does the passage name things that
  happened, or only things that are felt?
