# Taxonomy 13 — Foreshadowing & Symbolism Tells

**Sources for this cluster:** S03, S04, S28, S43, S44.
**Dominant causes:** K4 (taught device = default device), K3 (explain the meaning), K2 (no device budget).

> Note: this cluster is the least directly measured. Only FS02 and FS03 carry
> Medium confidence; FS01 is Low. All interventions here are capped at Level 3
> unless the author requests structural work.

---

## FS01 — Signposted foreshadowing

- **Definition.** Foreshadowing that announces itself: portent-heavy cues that flag
  the future event so loudly the surprise dies ("The ring felt strangely cold, as
  if it knew what was coming").
- **Example pattern.** Ominous emphasis on objects/persons who will matter later;
  the narrator's camera lingering on the gun.
- **Observable characteristics.** Portent-cue density; emphasis disproportionate
  to present relevance; dramatic-irony default.
- **Evidence.** Practitioner (S44); mechanism K4. No direct measurement.
  Confidence: **Low** — monitor; do not act automatically.
- **Likely cause.** K4: signposted foreshadowing is the taught, corpus-typical
  form; K3 wants the reader to "get it."
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in thriller/mystery (where some signposting is
  contract); most tell-like in literary.
- **Severity.** 1. **False-positive risk.** 3 — foreshadowing is craft; human
  writers signpost constantly and deliberately.
- **Effect on quality.** At saturation: reveals the machinery; the reader waits for
  the planted shoe to drop.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 2 only at clear saturation: prune the loudest
  cue; leave the quiet one. Otherwise no action.
- **Side effects.** Removing legitimately planted clues breaks mystery contracts.
- **Validation.** Saturation count + genre-contract check.

## FS02 — Rigid setup/payoff structure

- **Definition.** The text's plant/payoff ledger runs on schedule: everything
  planted pays off exactly once, at the expected point, with the expected weight
  (the structural face of N03).
- **Example pattern.** Chapter 1's detail returns in the climax with precisely
  the function it was planted for; no payoff surprises in *kind*, only in timing.
- **Observable characteristics.** Plant/payoff pairing regularity; S03's formulaic
  inner structure; S43's repeated plot elements.
- **Evidence.** S03 (Tier 0), S43 (Tier 0 secondary). Confidence: **Medium**.
- **Likely cause.** K4 + K2: the ledger is the corpus's canonical plot tool; the
  model can't plan *subversions* of it.
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in thriller/mystery; weakest in literary.
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 2.
- **Effect on quality.** Clockwork predictability (N03).
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 4 (structural): let one payoff arrive through
  an *unplanted* route or one plant pay off *wrongly* (the knife opens a letter).
  Author-gated.
- **Side effects.** Cheating the reader in mystery (the genre contract forbids
  unplanted solutions — exempt mystery).
- **Validation.** Ledger audit vs. genre norms.

## FS03 — In-text explanation of symbols/motifs

- **Definition.** The text decodes its own symbols: after the recurring image does
  its work, someone explains what it "represents."
- **Example pattern.** "The broken clock, she realized, had always been a symbol
  of her stalled life" — the symbol footnoted by the narrator.
- **Observable characteristics.** Symbol + gloss pairs; motifs that arrive with
  their interpretation attached; S28's explicitness; S04's subtext measure
  ("does subtext feel forced?").
- **Evidence.** S04 (Tier 0), S28 (Tier 0). Confidence: **Medium**.
- **Likely cause.** K3 + K4: interpreted fiction teaches symbol-plus-meaning;
  the model supplies both halves.
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in literary/coming-of-age; weakest in genre
  fiction (where symbols are rare anyway).
- **Severity.** 1. **False-positive risk.** 2.
- **Effect on quality.** The symbol stops working the moment it's explained;
  the reader's discovery is pre-empted.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 2: delete the gloss; the image already did the
  work (if it didn't, the fix is the image, not the gloss).
- **Side effects.** Symbols too private to decode (then simplify the image).
- **Validation.** Decodability check without the gloss.
