# Transformation Log — TIM-01 (Periodization / the Imperial Lifecycle)

Date: 2026-08-26
Canon target: **TIM-01** (the Kesra Era calendar; the five eras; the anchor events; the root-cause/trigger distinction)
Method: the 5-step sequential reasoning (the periodization applies the *imperial-lifecycle* mechanism from all the models to the Samur root causes).

## Step 1 — Historical examples and underlying mechanisms

- **Mughal** — the **succession-crisis trigger** (1707) that converts the built-up stresses (the jagirdari crisis, the Deccan overstretch) into a **fragmentation** into successor states.
- **Qing** — the **slow, compounding decline** (the demographic pressure + the fiscal rigidity + the banner decay) that no single trigger causes but every trigger accelerates.
- **Vijayanagara** — the **defeat that contracts, not ends, the state** (the rump; the institutional dispersal to the successors).
- **Maratha** — the **overstretch trigger** (Panipat, 1761) that stops the expansion and accelerates the sardar fragmentation.
- **Mysore / British** — the **late maritime/chartered trigger** (the Anglo-Mysore wars; the chartered company) that captures the economic center and adds a reverse-invasion axis.
- **All** — the **imperial lifecycle** as a **long process of interacting pressures** (fiscal + elite + environmental), with **root causes distinguished from trigger events**.

## Step 2 — Original historical conditions

- The Mughal 1707 succession war (the built-up stresses + the trigger).
- The Qing 18th–19th c. (the slow compounding decline).
- The Vijayanagara 1565 defeat (the contraction, the dispersal).
- The Maratha Panipat (the overstretch trigger).
- The Mysore/British (the late maritime/chartered trigger).

## Step 3 — Equivalent or contrasting Samur conditions

- The Samur **root causes** are the **six** built up over the High Empire + the Stress: the **three-front overstretch** (GEO-01), the **fiscal-military tension** (DEM-01), the **monetization chain** (GEO-01 §8.4), the **dual-elite fragilization** (DYN-01), the **succession fault line** (DYN-01), the **growth imperative** (DYN-01).
- The Samur **triggers** are the **five** late events: the **Great Succession Crisis** (~KE 520–560, the Mughal 1707 pattern), the **catastrophic wind-failure** (~KE 450–480), the **great epidemic** (~KE 460–490), the **Phre's arrival** (~KE 500–560, the Mysore/British pattern), **Vethra's rise** (~KE 550–650, the "state too strong").
- **Contrasts (the load-bearing differences):** the Samur decline is a **~120-year long process** (the Stress, KE 380–500) in which the **root causes build up first** and the **triggers act on them later** — the **Phre is a *completing* trigger, not a *causal* root** (unlike the Mughal, where the British are central to the decline, the Samur Phre arrives *after* the root causes have already broken the fiscal base). And the **calendar is anchored to the wind** (a failed wind is a calendar/event shock), which is a **Samur-specific** feature (the decline is *built into the timekeeping*).

## Step 4 — Original Samur solution

The **Kesra Era periodization** (the five eras + the anchor events + the root-cause/trigger distinction, with the **wind-anchored calendar**) is the **Samur-specific** imperial-lifecycle structure — calibrated to the Mughal/Qing/Vijayanagara/Maratha/Mysore-British lifecycles but shaped by the **Samur root causes** (the three-front overstretch, the monetization chain, the dual-elite fragilization, the succession fault line, the growth imperative) and the **Samur geography** (the wind, the Oren, the coast, the four borders). It is **not a reskin**: the **~120-year long Stress** (the root causes building up *before* the triggers) and the **wind-anchored calendar** (the decline built into the timekeeping) are the load-bearing differences.

## Step 5 — Consequences

- **Immediate:** the Kesra Era dating; the five eras; the anchor events (era-typical dates); the root-cause/trigger distinction.
- **Unintended:** the **wind-anchored calendar** makes a failed wind a **calendar/event shock**; the **long Stress** means the decline is a **~120-year process** (the empire is "declining" for a century before it "fragments").
- **Long-term:** the **periodization** is the skeleton for all later files; the **root-cause/trigger distinction** is the Phase 5 analytic tool; the **end of the House of Kesra (~KE 650–700)** is the horizon of the "Samur Empire" (the successor states are post-imperial).
