# Islam — Comparative Research

Status: RESEARCH
Date: 2026-08-26

Scope: the system as institutions — the ulema, the madhhab, the Sufi order, the waqf, and the ulema–sultan negotiation.

## 1. Theology as a system

- The core: **tawḥīd** (the oneness of God), the prophethood (a *final* prophet), the **Qur'ān as the fixed text**, the five pillars as the minimal practice. **The load-bearing institutional fact:** the **text is fixed and the fiqh (jurisprudence) is derived** — so the system generates an institutionalized *scholarly class* that produces law from a stable source, with the tension between **ijtihād** (independent reasoning) and **taqlīd** (following a school) as a standing internal dispute.
- The **Sunni–Shia division** (the succession after the Prophet) is **theological and political at once**: the **caliphate** (Sunni, consensual/political leadership) vs. the **Imamate** (Shia, a divinely-appointed line). It creates two distinct political theologies.

## 2. Institutions

1. **The madhhab and the fiqh.** The four Sunni schools (Ḥanafī, Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī) as the institutionalized jurisprudence; the **qāḍī** (the local judge, appointed by the ruler, trained in a madhhab); the **muftī** (the issuer of the fatwā). **Mechanism:** a **plural but bounded legal order** — the madhhab sets the frame, the qāḍī applies it locally, the muftī gives the authoritative interpretation. **Standing dispute:** the "gate of ijtihād" (the contested closure) — orthodoxy vs. reform.
2. **The ulema (the scholarly elite).** The class learned in the Qur'ān, the ḥadīth, and the fiqh; the **ḥadīth discipline** (collection, authentication, the *isnād* chain of transmission) as the epistemic method; the **madrasa** (e.g., the later Nizāmiyya) as the institutionalized training. **Mechanism:** a **self-reproducing scholarly class with its own credentials** (the *ijāza*, the chain of transmission) — a **parallel elite** to the military/nobility.
3. **The Sufi order (the tariqa).** The brotherhoods (Qādiriyya, Naqshbandiyya, etc.) as the **grassroots infrastructure**: the lodge (*khanqāh*), the master (*pir*), the disciple, the dhikr, the *baraka* (spiritual power), the shrine, the festival. **Mechanism:** a **mobile, non-territorial, grassroots institution that penetrates a society faster than the state or the ulema**; the Sufi is the *popular face* of the religion. **Political effect:** the tariqa as a **power broker** (the pir can mobilize the popular, legitimize the ruler, or oppose him).
4. **The waqf (the endowment).** Land/property dedicated to a pious purpose (the mosque, the madrasa, the hospital, the shrine, the family) and run by a **mutawallī** (manager). **Mechanism:** a **tax-exempt institutional property** that creates a **durable institutional base outside the state's direct control** — a parallel fiscal base — and a tool for the elite to secure family status.
5. **The caliphate / sultanate.** The **caliph** (the Sunni political-religious leader) vs. the **sultan** (the military ruler); the historical tension (the ʿAbbāsid caliph as religious legitimator, the Turkish sultan as military power). **Mechanism:** the **ulema–sultan negotiation** — the sultan needs the ulema's legitimation (the *khutba*, the fatwā), the ulema needs the sultan's protection and appointments (the qāḍī, the muftī).

## 3. Relationship to politics

- The **ulema–sultan symbiosis (with friction):** the sultan's power is legitimated by the ulema, but the sultan controls the ulema's appointments — a **structural tension** between the military-noble and the scholarly elite. The state **manages the Sufi** (patronage + the shrine's endowment) and **adopts an official madhhab** (e.g., the Ottoman Ḥanafī) to standardize the legal frame.

## 4. Internal disputes

- **Sunni–Shia** (the political-theological division; the basis of a separate Shia political tradition). The **madhhab** disputes and the "gate of ijtihād." The **Sufi-vs-ulema** tension (the popular vs. the scholarly; the charge of "Sufi excess"). The recurring **puritanical reform** movements.
