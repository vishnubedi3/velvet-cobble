## 1. Continental position — the continent of Veshran

The Samur Empire sits on **Veshran**, a subcontinent-scale landmass (roughly a **40-day march north–south, a 30-day march east–west** — an *estimate*, not false precision), bounded by **sea to the south and east** and by the **Khor steppe** and highland rims to the **north and north-west**. It is large enough to hold a core agrarian plain, a mountain wall, a steppe frontier, and a long coast — but not so large that a single interior power can ignore the sea.

The empire's center of gravity is the **Oren basin** (§2). The founding people, the **Samur**, are from the **upper Oren plateau** — the region just east of the Sareth and south of the Khel confluence — and the empire takes its name from them. The dynasty's legitimacy is tied to this heartland (to be developed in DYN-01, Phase 4).

## 2. The watershed system — the Oren

- **The Oren** is the great river: it rises in the **Sareth highlands** (west), flows **south** through the core plain, gathers two major tributaries, and fans into a broad **delta** on the **south coast**.
  - **The Khel** joins from the **west** (upper Oren).
  - **The Tarn** joins from the **east** (mid Oren) and drains the **Tarn plain** (the eastern periphery).
- **The Oren delta** is the empire's **southern port zone** — where the river meets the sea and the **seasonal wind** (§7) drives the maritime trade.
- **The Oren's flow is strongly seasonal** (§7): high in the wet season (flood + good navigation), low in the dry season (reduced navigation). This makes **river transport seasonal** and ties the empire's internal logistics to the calendar.

**Strategic logic:** the Oren is the empire's **spine** — the cheap long-distance logistics route, the revenue route (the grain moves down the Oren), and the **reverse-invasion route** (a power that controls the **delta mouth** can push up the Oren). Whoever holds the delta mouth holds the empire's trade *and* a reverse-invasion axis.

## 3. The strategic passes — the Sareth

- **The Sareth** is a **north–south mountain range** along the empire's **west** (roughly a 20-day march of its length). It is the empire's **western wall**.
- The Sareth has **few passes** — the main ones (named and numbered in GEO-02) are the **only viable routes through**, and each is a **chokepoint**: a small force can hold one against a large one.
- **Beyond the wall (west of the Sareth)** is a different world — the **Sareth outer zone** — the source of the empire's best **iron ore** and much of its **timber** (§5), but *outside* the core and therefore a **projection cost** (§8.1).
- **The heartland sits on the east side of the wall, on the Oren** — so the empire is **easy to defend from the west** (the wall + the passes) but **expensive to project west or north** (through the passes, into the open steppe). This is the **defensible-core / projection-problem**.

## 4. Agricultural zones — bār and pell

The empire's food is a **dual-crop structure** divided by climate and water:

- **Bār** — a hardy **dry-summer cereal** (the "bread grain"; the tax grain). Grown in the **upper and mid Oren basin** (the irrigated / dry-summer zone, the core). This is the **fiscal heartland** — the grain that is assessed, taxed, and moved down the Oren.
- **Pell** — a **wet-land paddy** (the "wet grain"). Grown in the **Oren delta**, the **Tarn plain** (east), and the **Veth shore** (south), where the water is year-round (wind-fed / paddy). This is the **denser, richer, but more remote** zone.

**The core–periphery food divide:** the **bār core** (upper Oren) is the fiscal and political heartland; the **pell periphery** (delta + east + Veth) is denser and richer in trade but **remote** (separated by water and distance) and therefore **hard to administer by land** → the site of tributary control and later fragmentation (§8.3). A power that rules the bār core but cannot administer the pell periphery is a **composite monarchy**.

## 5. Resource scarcities — where each scarce resource lies

The core (the Oren basin) has the **food surplus and the labor** but **lacks** several critical resources, each lying on a **different border** — so the empire must **project in several directions** to obtain them. This is the load-bearing structural feature (see the transformation log §4).

| Resource | Where it lies | Why scarce in the core | The projection it forces |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Horses** | the **Khor steppe** (north) only | the agrarian core breeds poor horses | a **north** frontier — trade with / raid on the Khor; a **permanent** strategic dependency |
| **Iron (best ore)** | the **Sareth foothills** (west) + a **southern highland** (near Vethra) | the Oren basin has some, but the *best* ore is at the margins | a **west** projection (through the Sareth passes) + a **south** projection |
| **Timber** | the **Sareth forests** (west) + the **Khor highlands** (north) | the Oren basin is **tree-poor** (deforestation; fuel, construction, ship-timber scarce) | a **west** + a **north** projection; a major transport cost |
| **Salt** | the **Khor salt-flats** (north) + **coastal pans** (south / delta) | the interior has little | a **north** + a **south/east** projection; a **state-monopoly candidate** |
| **Silver (the monetary metal)** | the **Khor highlands** (north) + a **western Sareth mine** | the core is silver-poor | a **north** + a **west** projection; **funds the monetization chain** (§8.4) |
| **Coastal trade goods** (the Veth pepper and spice analogues, fine textiles) | the **Veth shore** + the **Oren delta** | the interior is landlocked from the sea | a **south / maritime** projection; the **coast is the trade wealth** |

**Key structural fact:** the three *most* strategically scarce resources — **horses (north), timber/iron (west), silver (north + west)** — lie on **several different borders**, so **no single frontier dominates**. The empire must hold the Khor, the Sareth, *and* the coast **simultaneously**.

## 6. Natural barriers

- **The Sareth** (west) — a mountain wall; the empire's best defensive barrier and its iron/timber source.
- **The Khor** (north) — the **open steppe / highland; no wall.** The north is **defensible only by presence** (a standing army), not by terrain — the **hard frontier**.
- **The sea** (south / east) — the coast is open to **maritime** powers; the **delta mouth** is the key chokepoint (whoever holds it controls the Oren trade and the reverse-invasion axis).
- **The delta / the Tarn plain's water** (south / east) — the paddy periphery is **crossed by water**, making it **hard to administer by land** (a power must project by river / by sea to govern it).

## 7. Climate and the seasonal wind

- The empire has a **seasonal wind** (a monsoon-like wind): a **wet season** (the **sea-wind** brings rain from the south/east onto the coast and, up the Oren, the plains) and a **dry season** (the **land-wind**, dry and dusty).
- **Consequences:**
  - **The Oren runs high in the wet season** (flood risk + good navigation) and **low in the dry season** (reduced navigation).
  - **Land campaigns** are a **dry-season** activity (firm ground; steppe forage available).
  - **River / sea logistics** are a **wet-season** activity (high water; the sea-wind for sailing).
  - **The paddy (pell) cycle** is tied to the wet season (planting and harvest fixed by the wind).
  - **This seasonality is a hard constraint on the empire's military calendar** (MIL-01) and its **fiscal calendar** (the tax is gathered at the harvest; the campaign season is the dry season).

## 8. Strategic consequences — the six mechanisms the geography forces

1. **The defensible core + the projection problem.** The Sareth makes the west easy to defend but expensive to project; the Khor is open (defensible only by presence); the coast is open to the sea. → a **standing three-front fiscal-military condition**.
2. **The standing frontier (not a single war).** The Khor (horses) + the Sareth (iron/timber) + the coast (trade) are **permanent** conditions. The empire's fiscal base (the Oren bār) must feed a **standing military presence on three fronts** → the **fiscal-military tension**.
3. **The tributary periphery (the fragmentation site).** The **Tarn plain / eastern delta** is fertile, high-revenue, but **remote** (separated by water and distance) and **hard to administer** → central control is **nominal (tribute)** and local elites are strong → the **site of composite-monarchy fragmentation** (successor states inherit the toolkit).
4. **The monetization chain.** The cash-revenue state needs **silver**, which comes from the **Khor + the Sareth** → the **frontier trade funds the monetization** → the **cash-crop / moneylender / peasant-debt** chain operates in the Oren basin → **revolt when the targets inflate**.
5. **The "state too strong for its size" neighbor.** **Vethra**, on the **south coast**, is **compact, well-irrigated, craft-guild-rich, and strategically valuable** (the coast + the delta + the pepper). It is **too strong for its size** → the Samur Empire (and later the maritime power) will try to **contain** it → a **standing rivalry**.
6. **The maritime-commercial power's access.** The **Oren delta + the Veth shore** are the ports; the **seasonal wind** makes the **sea-lanes seasonal**; a distant maritime power (**the Phre**) projects through a **chartered trading company** that operates from **delta enclaves / coastal ports** — with **naval / river logistics as the binding constraint** (it can hold the ports + the delta mouth, but the **interior is beyond its riverine reach**). This sets up the **late, transformative force** (Phase 5).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the empire's **fiscal base** is the **Oren bār core**; its **military calendar** is the **dry-season campaign / wet-season logistics**; its **trade** is the **delta + the Veth coast**; its **scarce resources** force **multi-directional projection**.
- **Unintended:**
  - The **defensible core** encourages **inward-looking defense** and **costly outward projection** → a **standing overstretch** when the empire holds all three fronts at once.
  - The **tributary east** (the Tarn plain) becomes a **successor-state incubator** — the fragmentation site.
  - The **monetization chain** (silver from the frontier → cash in the core) makes the **peasant** dependent on the **moneylender** and the **frontier** (a **revolt** when the targets inflate).
  - The **Vethra** containment rivalry becomes a **standing drain** on the empire's southern resources.
  - The **Phre chartered company** enters through the **delta** and, over time, makes the **delta mouth** its **power-base** (a reverse-invasion axis up the Oren).
- **Long-term:** the **three-front condition** is a **root cause** of the empire's eventual **fiscal-military overstretch** (Phase 5); the **tributary-east fragmentation** is the **post-imperial pattern** (successor states); the **monetization chain** is the **social fault line** (peasant / moneylender / state); the **Vethra + the Phre** are the **two external pressures** the decline interacts with.
