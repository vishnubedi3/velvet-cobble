> DYN-01 is the dynasty as a **structure** (the founding, the legitimacy, the succession, the factions) — **not** yet a *history* (the individual kings and their dates are TIM-01 + the later DYN files). Titles and names are provisional (fixed in CUL-01).

## 1. Origin (the material founding)

The **Samur** are a **horse-poor, agrarian-military people** of the **upper Oren plateau** (the heartland: east of the Sareth, south of the Khel confluence — GEO-02 §1). Their **founding advantage is position, not numbers**: they sit at the **junction of**

- **the Sareth passes** (the west wall; the iron/timber source beyond it),
- **the upper Oren** (the river; the logistics spine), and
- **the bār heartland** (the fiscal base; the tax grain).

They rise by **controlling the chokepoint** — the Sareth passes + the upper Oren — i.e., by becoming the power that controls the **west trade (iron/timber) + the river logistics + the fiscal base**. This is a **material founding** (control of the chokepoint + the fiscal base), not a purely military conquest. The **founder, Kesra I**, unifies the bār core from this position; the capital **Kesra** bears his name (GEO-02 §2).

## 2. The unification (how the core was consolidated)

Kesra I unifies the bār core by three material moves:

1. **Absorbing the local chiefs** (the pre-existing agrarian-military aristocracy of the upper Oren) into a **revenue-military chief system** — the chiefs keep their **districts in revenue assignment** with a **military-service obligation** (the Vijayanagara nayaka/dandanayaka logic). The chiefs become the empire's **local power base** (the "chiefs" faction).
2. **Controlling the Oren** (the river logistics) — the grain moves down the Oren, so the unifier controls the **fiscal spine** (Besra, the grain market, sits on it — GEO-02 §2).
3. **Co-opting the temple** (the **Veshna** temple, the founding faith's center — GEO-02 §2) — the unifier becomes the temple's **patron/protector**, and the **temple sanctions the king** (the consecration, the ritual legitimation). The king is the **protector of dharma** (the Sanatan king-as-protector logic).

## 3. The founding institutions (the dual structure + the sanctioner)

The founding creates a **dual structure** with a **sanctioning institution**:

1. **The King (the Samur royal house / the House of Kesra)** — the **legitimacy**: the heartland, the temple, the founding myth, the protector of dharma. The **symbolic/ritual center**.
2. **The Shreshtha (the prime-ministerial / regent office)** — the **power**: the army, the revenue, the administration. The **bureaucratic-military center**. This is a **founding office** (not an accident or a usurpation) — the founding *required both* a legitimate house (the Samur, the heartland, the temple) *and* a military-administrative center (the Shreshtha, the army, the revenue). The two are **institutionalized as separate but interlocked offices from the start**.
   - **The Shreshtha's office is a *salaried, non-hereditary* office** (the Mughal mansab logic) — the Shreshtha's family does **not** automatically inherit it; the Shreshtha **depends on the king's continuous favor** (the court as the only source of wealth, the Mughal logic).
3. **The Temple (the Veshna matha / the priestly council)** — the **sanctioner**: the **source of legitimacy** that **consecrates the king** (the ritual that makes a king "legitimate") and **balances the court** (the legitimation, the fiscal sink of the temple economy, the mobile node of the matha). The temple is **not a co-equal office** but the **arbitrator/sanctioner** that can **break the tie** — and **de-legitimize**.

**The load-bearing structural difference (vs. a reskin):** the **dual structure (the King + the Shreshtha) is a *founding feature*** — institutionalized from the start, **not** a temporary usurpation (the Mysore Hyder/Tipu) **nor** an adoption accident (the Maratha Peshwa) — and the **temple is the *sanctioner*** (the consecration) that **arbitrates between the King and the Shreshtha**. This triadic interlock (the King + the Shreshtha + the Temple as sanctioner) is **structurally novel** — not a direct reskin of the Mughal (the court + the ulema), the Maratha (the Peshwa + the sardar), or the Qing (the banner + the li-bu).

## 4. The succession system (the fault line)

- **The King is chosen from the royal house** (the Samur agnates) — a **qualified elective-hereditary** system: the **council of the house + the temple** chooses the **most worthy** of the royal agnates, with a **preference for the eldest but not an absolute rule** (not strict primogeniture).
- **The fault line:** the **"eldest"** (the primogenitor's claim) vs. the **"most worthy"** (the military-able claim) — and the **temple** and the **Shreshtha** both have a role in the choice, so the **succession is a three-way contest** (the royal house's claim, the Shreshtha's preference, the temple's sanction).
- **This seeds the later succession crisis** — a **contested succession** (the "eldest vs. the most worthy" + the three-way contest) that **fragments the empire** (the Mughal 1707 pattern).

## 5. The elite factions (the five)

1. **The dynasty (the House of Kesra)** — the legitimacy; the claim to the throne; the "eldest vs. the most worthy" fault line.
2. **The Shreshtha (the prime-ministerial / regent center)** — the bureaucratic-military power; the salaried, non-hereditary office; depends on the king's favor; the court.
3. **The temple (the Veshna matha / the priestly council)** — the religious institution; the sanctioner (the consecration); the fiscal sink (the temple economy); the mobile node (the matha); the legitimation; the **kingmaker**.
4. **The chiefs (the agrarian-military aristocracy, the absorbed nayaka)** — the regional power base; the **hereditary-in-practice districts**; the local power; the **successor-state incubator** (the fragmentation site).
5. **The merchant/banker class (the war financiers)** — the war finance; the **growth imperative** (the debt-financed war must keep conquering, the shahukar logic); the urban/commercial power (Besra, Voren).

**The dual elite (the founding/absorbed duality):** the **founding elite** (the Samur warrior band — the royal house + the Shreshtha's office + the military houses) is **institutionally distinct** from the **absorbed elite** (the pre-existing agrarian-military chiefs, the co-opted administrators). This is the **Qing banner/li-bu dual structure**, but the **load-bearing difference**: the duality is by **founding status** (the founding Samur vs. the absorbed chiefs), **not by ethnicity** (not the Qing Manchu/Han). The **founding elite** will **fragilize** (the banner's decay, the Qing logic) while the **absorbed elite** (the chiefs) will **accumulate power** (the regionalization).

## 6. The sources of legitimacy (the bundle)

The dynasty's legitimacy is a **bundle** (not a single source):

- **The heartland** (the Samur, the upper Oren plateau — the founding people/region; "we are the Samur").
- **The temple** (the Veshna — the consecration; the protector of dharma; the ritual legitimation).
- **The military** (the founding conquest; the army; the control of the Sareth passes).
- **The fiscal base** (the bār core; the Oren; the grain; the revenue).

**The fault line:** the **temple** (the ritual/symbolic) vs. the **military/fiscal** (the material). When the **temple's sanction** and the **military/fiscal reality** diverge (a strong military Shreshtha who lacks the temple's full sanction, or a temple-backed king who lacks the military/fiscal control), the **legitimacy fractures** (the later succession crisis).

## 7. The long-term consequences (seeded by the founding)

- **The King vs. the Shreshtha** — the **legitimacy vs. the power** fault line (the Mysore/Maratha pattern) — the Shreshtha will **accumulate power** and the dynasty will **resent** it; when a weak king rises, the Shreshtha will **dominate** (the Maratha Peshwa logic).
- **The succession fault line** — the "eldest vs. the most worthy" + the **three-way contest** (the house, the Shreshtha, the temple) — a **contested succession** will **fragment the empire** (the Mughal 1707 pattern).
- **The chiefs' hereditary districts** — the **regional power base** that becomes the **successor-state incubator** (the Vijayanagara nayaka fragmentation).
- **The temple's sanction** — the **legitimation** that can **break the tie** in a succession (the consecration) — the temple becomes a **kingmaker** (and a **target** when the court tries to control it).
- **The merchant/banker growth imperative** — the **debt-financed war** that must keep conquering (the shahukar) — the **fiscal-military overstretch** (the Phase 5 decline).
- **The dual elite (the founding Samur vs. the absorbed chiefs)** — the **founding elite** will **fragilize** (the Qing banner decay) while the **absorbed elite** (the chiefs) will **accumulate power** (the regionalization).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the **dual founding** (the King + the Shreshtha, with the temple as sanctioner); the **five elite factions**; the **qualified elective-hereditary succession**; the **composite legitimacy** (the heartland + the temple + the military + the fiscal base).
- **Unintended:** the **King vs. Shreshtha** fault line (the legitimacy vs. the power); the **succession three-way contest** (the house, the Shreshtha, the temple); the **chiefs' hereditary districts** (the regional power base); the **temple as kingmaker** (and target); the **merchant/banker growth imperative** (the debt-financed war).
- **Long-term:** the **contested succession** fragments the empire (the Mughal 1707 pattern); the **chiefs** become the **successor states** (the Vijayanagara nayaka fragmentation); the **founding elite fragilizes** while the **absorbed elite accumulates** (the Qing banner/li-bu decay); the **merchant/banker growth imperative** drives the **fiscal-military overstretch** (the Phase 5 decline).
