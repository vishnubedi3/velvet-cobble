# Taxonomy 12 — Conflict & Tension Tells

**Sources for this cluster:** S03, S37, S43, S44.
**Dominant causes:** K3 (resolution preference, moral legibility), K7 (sanitization), K4 (template conflicts).

---

## F01 — Predictable escalation ladder

- **Definition.** Conflict escalates along a fixed ladder — small obstacle, bigger
  obstacle, biggest obstacle, final boss — each rung larger but identical in kind.
- **Example pattern.** Three increasingly dangerous henchmen before the villain;
  obstacles that differ only in magnitude, never in nature.
- **Observable characteristics.** Obstacle-type monotonicity; escalation without
  complication (no lateral moves, no changing the game); S43's repeated plot
  elements.
- **Evidence.** S43 (Tier 0 secondary), practitioner (S44). Confidence: **Medium**.
- **Likely cause.** K4: escalation ladders are the corpus's canonical plot;
  K2 can't plan a *change of kind*.
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in thriller/action; weakest in literary.
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 2 — ladders are a genre contract in
  thriller; the tell is the *unvarying* ladder.
- **Effect on quality.** Predictable pressure; the reader prices in each rung.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 4: change the conflict's *kind* at one rung
  (physical → social → internal → informational) per the story model's conflict
  register.
- **Side effects.** Tonally jarring shifts if unmotivated.
- **Validation.** Obstacle-type audit over the draft.

## F02 — Artificial misunderstanding, instantly cleared

- **Definition.** Conflict manufactured from a misunderstanding that real people
  would resolve in one sentence, resolved exactly when the plot needs the next beat.
- **Example pattern.** The overheard half-sentence; the unopened letter; the
  misread gesture — all cleared the moment the misunderstanding has done its plot
  job.
- **Observable characteristics.** Misunderstanding-driven conflict density; trivial
  information asymmetries; instant clearing.
- **Evidence.** Practitioner-strong (S44); mechanism K4 (soap-opera templates) +
  K3 (fast resolution). Confidence: **Medium**.
- **Likely cause.** K4 + K3: the misunderstanding is the corpus's cheapest conflict
  generator and the model's preference for resolution ends it fast.
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in romance/drama; weakest in noir (where
  misunderstandings are deliberate).
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 2 — miscommunication is real human
  material; the tell is the *trivial* version.
- **Effect on quality.** Conflict without substance; characters who don't talk like
  people.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 4: replace the trivial misunderstanding with a
  *substantive* one (a real difference in values or interests) or make the
  misunderstanding irresolvable-by-talking (pride, stakes, history).
- **Side effects.** Over-engineered conflict where lightness was the tone.
- **Validation.** "Why don't they just talk?" test — the answer must be a *reason*,
  not the plot's convenience.

## F03 — Moral clarity / good-vs-evil flattening

- **Definition.** The conflict's moral structure is fully legible: one side right,
  one side wrong; the antagonist's motives are evil-by-default; justice is
  administered exactly.
- **Example pattern.** The villain's goal is domination and nothing else; the
  hero's cause is unalloyed; the ending punishes the wicked and rewards the good.
- **Observable characteristics.** Absence of moral remainder (N07's conflict-side
  face); S03's alignment effects; S37's reduced negative affect.
- **Evidence.** S03 (Tier 0), S37 (Tier 1). Confidence: **Medium-High**.
- **Likely cause.** K3 + K7: alignment produces morally legible worlds by design.
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in fantasy/YA; weakest (and most tell-like) in
  noir/literary.
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 1.
- **Effect on quality.** Fable-level stakes; villains without reasons; no genuine
  moral questions.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 3–5: give the antagonist one interest that is
  *comprehensible* (not justified — comprehensible) and let one consequence be
  unjust. Author-gated (worldview territory).
- **Side effects.** Forced moral ambiguity (see N07).
- **Validation.** Author-intent check (PV-13) + expert read.

## F04 — Premature resolution

- **Definition.** Conflicts resolve at their first structural opportunity; nothing
  is allowed to fester past its usefulness.
- **Example pattern.** The feud ends in chapter 4; the investigation solves itself
  mid-book; every tension has a built-in expiry.
- **Observable characteristics.** Conflict-lifespan distribution at minimum;
  S03's weak turning points; T05 is the pacing-side twin.
- **Evidence.** S03 (Tier 0), S43. Confidence: **Medium**.
- **Likely cause.** K3: resolution preference; unresolved states read as incomplete.
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in drama/romance; weakest in serial/episodic.
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 1.
- **Effect on quality.** No accumulating pressure; the story deflates between
  set-pieces.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 4: defer one resolution; let the wound stay
  open through several scenes (R03's relationship-side twin).
- **Side effects.** Drawn conflict without development = stalling.
- **Validation.** Conflict-lifespan audit.

## F05 — Sanitized conflict; softened stakes

- **Definition.** The conflict's costs are softened: violence is offscreen and
  consequence-free, cruelty is implied not shown, and negative stakes are buffered.
- **Example pattern.** The battle leaves no one maimed; the betrayal leaves no
  lasting damage; the villain's cruelty is mentioned, never witnessed.
- **Observable characteristics.** Reduced anger/sadness/aggression language (S37);
  consequence-free conflict; S03's uniformly positive arcs.
- **Evidence.** S37 (Tier 1, direct affect reduction), S03 (Tier 0). Confidence:
  **Medium-High**.
- **Likely cause.** K7: safety training suppresses violent/negative content;
  fiction inherits the guardrails.
- **Variation.** Genre: strongest in horror/thriller prompts (where it fights the
  genre); weakest in cozy (where it *is* the genre).
- **Severity.** 2. **False-positive risk.** 3 — restraint is a legitimate aesthetic
  (implied horror can be stronger); the tell is sanitization *against the story's
  own stakes*.
- **Effect on quality.** Unearned stakes; danger the reader never believes.
- **Recommended mitigation.** Level 4: let one consequence be *shown* and *stick*
  (the injury that persists, the loss that isn't repaired). Author-gated; respect
  the work's content boundaries — this is authorial territory above all.
- **Side effects.** Gratuitousness.
- **Validation.** Author-content-consent check first (PV-14), then stakes-belief
  read.
