# Transformation Log — DYN-01 (The Founding Dynasty)

Date: 2026-08-26
Canon target: **DYN-01** (the Samur founding; the dual structure — the King + the Shreshtha, with the temple as sanctioner; the qualified elective-hereditary succession; the five elite factions; the composite legitimacy; the founding-status dual elite)
Method: the 5-step sequential reasoning.

## Step 1 — Historical examples and underlying mechanisms

- **Mughal** — the court as the state's center; the **salaried, non-hereditary office** (the mansab); the court as the only source of wealth; the **succession crisis (1707)** that fragments the empire.
- **Maratha** — the **Peshwa regent** above the dynastic line (the adoption); the **Peshwa vs. the sardar** (centralization vs. autonomy).
- **Qing** — the **dual elite / dual household** (the banner + the li-bu); the co-opted class supervised by the conqueror's class; the **banner's fragilization**.
- **Vijayanagara** — the **agrarian-military aristocracy** (the nayaka/dandanayaka); the chief as the local power base; the **hereditary-in-practice district**; the **fragmentation into successor states**.
- **Mysore** — the **dynastic legitimacy vs. the military-administrative control** (the Wodeyar vs. the Hyder/Tipu).
- **Sanatan** — the **king as protector of dharma**; the **temple** as economic institution + legitimation; the **guru/matha** as the mobile node; the **consecration**.

## Step 2 — Original historical conditions

- The Mughal court (the salaried mansab, the court as the wealth source, the 1707 succession war).
- The Maratha Peshwa (the regent above the dynastic line, the sardar).
- The Qing banner (the conqueror's class, the co-opted class).
- The Vijayanagara nayaka (the agrarian-military chief, the district).
- The Mysore Wodeyar / Hyder-Tipu (the dynasty vs. the military house).
- The Sanatan temple (the consecration, the protector of dharma, the matha).

## Step 3 — Equivalent or contrasting Samur conditions

- The Samur founding is a **material founding** (control of the Sareth passes + the upper Oren + the bār core — the chokepoint + the fiscal base), not a purely military conquest.
- The **dual structure** (the King + the Shreshtha) is a **founding feature** (institutionalized from the start), **not** an accident (the Maratha adoption) **or** an usurpation (the Mysore Hyder/Tipu).
- The **temple (the Veshna)** is the **sanctioner** (the consecration) that **arbitrates** between the King and the Shreshtha — not merely a legitimator (the Mughal ulema) but an **arbitrator/kingmaker**.
- The **succession** is a **qualified elective-hereditary** system (the house + the temple choose the most worthy, with a preference for the eldest) — a **three-way contest** (the house, the Shreshtha, the temple).
- The **dual elite** (the founding Samur vs. the absorbed chiefs) is by **founding status**, **not** by ethnicity (not the Qing Manchu/Han).

**Contrasts (the load-bearing differences):** the **dual structure is a *founding feature*** (not an accident or an usurpation); the **temple is the *sanctioner*** (the consecration, the arbitrator/kingmaker); the **dual elite is by *founding status*** (not ethnicity); the **succession is a *three-way contest*** (the house, the Shreshtha, the temple).

## Step 4 — Original Samur solution

The **Samur founding** — the material control of the chokepoint + the fiscal base; the absorption of the chiefs; the co-option of the temple; the **dual structure** (the King + the Shreshtha, with the temple as sanctioner); the **qualified elective-hereditary succession** (the three-way contest); the **five elite factions** — is the **Samur-specific answer** to the **Samur conditions** (the heartland's position at the Sareth/Oren/bār junction; the horse-poor agrarian-military people; the pre-existing chiefs; the temple). It is **not a reskin**: the **dual founding (the King + the Shreshtha, with the temple as sanctioner)** and the **founding-status dual elite** are the load-bearing differences.

## Step 5 — Consequences

- **Immediate:** the dual founding; the five factions; the qualified elective-hereditary succession; the composite legitimacy.
- **Unintended:** the King vs. Shreshtha fault line; the succession three-way contest; the chiefs' hereditary districts; the temple as kingmaker (and target); the merchant/banker growth imperative.
- **Long-term:** the contested succession fragments the empire (the Mughal 1707 pattern); the chiefs become the successor states (the Vijayanagara nayaka fragmentation); the founding elite fragilizes while the absorbed elite accumulates (the Qing banner/li-bu decay); the merchant/banker growth imperative drives the fiscal-military overstretch (the Phase 5 decline).
