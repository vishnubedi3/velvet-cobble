> DEM-02 fixes the **demographic structure** (resolves Q-006, Q-009, Q-010, Q-011): the city sizes/tiering, the urban/rural split, the sex/age structure, and the epidemic natural history. Ranges are **plausible, not false precision** (the project rule, DEM-01).

## 1. The population by era (anchors consistent with TIM-02)

- **Founding/Expansion (KE 0–220):** ~25–30M.
- **High Empire (KE 220–380):** peak ~**35M** (the good-wind decades, the Varan I era, TIM-02).
- **Stress (KE 380–500):** the Great Famine (KE 455–470) + the Great Epidemic (KE 460–480) → ~**28M** by KE 480 (a ~10–15% contraction, §4).
- **Fragmentation (KE 500–680):** ~**25M** (the divided empire, the Vethra's rise, the Phre's enclave).

## 3. The sex/age structure (resolves Q-010)

- **The sex ratio:** ~**1.0** among adults (slightly male-skewed in the martial castes, the garrisons).
- **The mortality:** ~**40% of children die before age 5** (a plausible pre-industrial range); life expectancy ~**35–40 at birth**, ~**55–60 if reaching adulthood**.
- **The age structure (the High-Empire peak):** under 14 ~**30%**; working-age (14–50) ~**55%**; 50+ ~**15%**.
- **The military recruit pool:** males 16–40 in the agrarian zones ≈ **3–4M** (the ~70–80% agrarian share, DEM-01); the standing army is a **small fraction** of that (MIL-02: ~35–45k standing, 80–110k full mobilization — a ~0.3% standing rate).
- **The dependent ratio:** ~**0.6** non-workers per worker → the **corvée capacity** (the 20–40 days/household/year, ECO-02) and the **labor-supply constraint** (the DEM-01 §5 fiscal-military geometry).

## 4. The epidemic natural history (resolves Q-011)

The "plague" is a **disease complex**, not a single pathogen (the medieval disease ecology, the Oren/sea vectors):

| Disease | Vector | Where | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The river-sickness** | water-borne (the Oren low-water, the delta) | the delta, the pell coast | cholera-like; the low-water season |
| **The port-fever** | trade-borne (the sea-lanes, the Voren) | the Voren, the pell port | plague-like; the maritime trade; **from KE 500 the Phre routes add the vector** |
| **The hunger-fever** | famine-borne (the refugees) | the bār core (the refugees) | typhus-like; the post-famine years |

- **The Great Epidemic (KE 460–480, TIM-02) = the compound:** the Great Famine (KE 455–470) → the refugee flow to the bār core → the **port-fever + the hunger-fever overlap** → the ~**10–15% contraction over ~20 years** (~28M by KE 480, §1).
- **The frequency:** a moderate port-fever epidemic every ~**20–40 years** (the Voren); a great (compound) epidemic every ~**80–150 years** (the last: KE 460–480).
- **The vector ecology:** the **Oren highway + the sea-lanes + the migration + the urban density + the temple festivals** (the mass gatherings — the festivals accelerate transmission **but** the Veshna rituals also **structure the quarantine response** — the shrines as the isolation, the rite as the protocol; the temple as the public-health institution, REL-02).
- **The social impact:** the **legitimacy blow** (the protector-of-dharma fails — TIM-02), the **labor collapse** (the fiscal-military system, DEM-01 §5), the **temple economy** (the matha's response cements its authority — a REL-02 consequence).
- **Alternatives considered:** a single named pathogen (rejected — the medieval disease complex is more plausible and more causally useful); a supernatural plague (rejected — the world is historical/material, per the mission).

## 5. The migration (the demographic flows)

- **The famine → the core:** the failed-wind years push the pell labor **into the bār core** (the core is spared — it takes the refugees; the epidemic risk, §4).
- **The famine → the Vethra:** the Vethra **absorbs the pell refugees** in the failed-wind years (a **Vethra growth mechanism** — the Vethra's rise is partly a demographic absorption, TIM-03).
- **The marriage migration:** the Tarn houses marry into the core (the DYN-03 royal/chief marriages) — the **Tarn-core bond** (and its failure in the crisis, TIM-03).
- **The port pull:** the Voren grows by the **maritime trade pull** (the merchants, the sailors — the urban share, §2).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the population by era (the anchors), the city tiering (the sizes), the urban/rural split (the 10–12% peak), the sex/age structure (the recruit pool, the corvée capacity), the epidemic complex (the three diseases, the Great Epidemic's compound mechanism), the migration (the famine flows, the Vethra absorption, the marriage migration).
- **Unintended:** the **Vethra's rise is partly a demographic absorption** (the Vethra grows by taking the Samur's failed-wind refugees — a negative-space insight: the Vethra's "too big" is also a **demographic** phenomenon, not just an economic one); the **temple as the public-health institution** (the festivals vector the disease **and** the rituals structure the response — the matha's authority is cemented by its epidemic response, REL-02).
- **Long-term:** the **demographic cycle** (the good-wind boom, the great-failure contraction — GEO-03 §2) is the **environmental root-cause cadence** of the Stress (TIM-01 root cause #3); the **recruit pool (3–4M)** is the **ceiling on the army** (MIL-02 — the army can never exceed a ~0.3% standing rate without breaking the labor supply).
