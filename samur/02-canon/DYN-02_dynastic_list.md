> DYN-02 is the **dynastic list** — the specific kings of the House of Kesra, the Shreshthas, the consecration ritual, and the personal-name pool. It names the agents that TIM-02 references anonymously (the weak king, the Shreshtha who dominates, the contested claimants). The kings' reigns are consistent with TIM-02's dated events. **Resolves Q-012, Q-014, Q-017, Q-037, Q-041, Q-056, Q-057.**

## 1. The personal-name pool (resolves Q-041)

The Samur-language personal names are **consonant-rich** (the CUL-01 register). Three classes:

- **The place-name doubling** (the Samur naming convention — place names double as personal names): **Kesra, Veshna, Besra, Threna, Gheshar, Voren, Kesveth, Keshkhor, Tarn, Sareth, Khel, Oren**.
- **The royal/elite -an/-ar names:** **Sharan, Dharan, Vethan, Kharan, Varan, Peshan, Ghoran, Zharan, Maren, Neshan, Keshan, Taren, Orenar, Sarethan**.
- **The -esh/-ath names:** **Tarnesh, Khesh, Rhaesh, Dhresh, Kesath, Veshath, Threnath**.

**The name-pool rule (for the later narrative phase, if authorized):** all personal names must come from this pool. No invented names without a pool entry.

## 2. The House of Kesra — the kings (resolves Q-017, Q-056)

**The dynasty runs KE 0 to ~KE 680 (~680 years), 14 named kings (with two concurrent rival kings in the Fragmentation). Average reign ~48 years.** The kings' reigns are consistent with TIM-02's dated events.

### Era 1 — The Founding (0–60)

**Kesra I (the Founder, KE 0–35)**
- **Character:** the unifier — the material founding (the chokepoint + the fiscal base), the absorption of the chiefs, the co-option of Veshna, the establishment of the dual structure.
- **Key events:** the Founding (KE 0, TIM-02).
- **Succession:** dies KE 35. The **first succession** (Kesra I → Kesra II, KE ~30–60) is **without a crisis** (the system works — the three-way contest is managed, the temple and the Shreshtha concur).

**Kesra II (the Consolidator, KE 35–72)**
- **Character:** the consolidator — secures the bār core, institutionalizes the chief system and the revenue, begins the projection to the borders.
- **Key events:** the first succession (KE ~35, TIM-02); the start of the Expansion.
- **Succession:** dies KE 72. Succeeded by Veshna I (the "most worthy" agnate, the system works).

### Era 2 — The Expansion (60–220)

**Veshna I (the Expander, KE 72–120)**
- **Character:** the expander — the first projection to the four borders (the Sareth west, the Khor north).
- **Key events:** the Frontiers (KE ~80–180, TIM-02) — Threna garrisoned, Keshkhor founded.
- **Succession:** dies KE 120. Succeeded by Sharan I.

**Sharan I (the Frontier King, KE 120–175)**
- **Character:** the frontier king — the Tarn east (the tributary), the Veth south (the first contact).
- **Key events:** the Frontiers (KE ~120–180, TIM-02) — the Tarn tributary established, Kesveth founded.
- **Succession:** dies KE 175. Succeeded by Kharan I.

**Kharan I (the First Vethra-War King, KE 175–225)**
- **Character:** the first Vethra-war king — the first containment of the Vethra (the "state too big").
- **Key events:** the First Vethra War (KE ~200–240, TIM-02); Voren founded (KE ~150–200).
- **Succession:** dies KE 225. Succeeded by Dharan I.

### Era 3 — The High Empire (220–380)

**Dharan I (the Great-Builder King, KE 225–290)**
- **Character:** the great-builder king — the Veshna great temple, the Oren irrigation/roads, the expansion of Kesra.
- **Key events:** the Great Building Era (KE ~220–380, TIM-02).
- **Succession:** dies KE 290. Succeeded by Varan I.

**Varan I (the Peak Emperor, KE 290–345)**
- **Character:** the peak emperor — the fiscal peak, the population peak, the High Empire at its height.
- **Key events:** the Great Building Era (KE ~220–380, TIM-02) — the fiscal/population peak.
- **Succession:** dies KE 345. Succeeded by Peshan I.

**Peshan I (the Overstretch King, KE 345–385)**
- **Character:** the overstretch king — the three-front condition begins to strain the fiscal base; the first failed-wind famine; the chiefs begin to accumulate power.
- **Key events:** the First Overstretch (KE ~340–380, TIM-02) — the turning point.
- **Succession:** dies KE 385. Succeeded by Ghoran I.

### Era 4 — The Stress (380–500)

**Ghoran I (the Revolt King, KE 385–430)**
- **Character:** the revolt king — the monetization chain breaks; the first major peasant revolt.
- **Key events:** the First Major Peasant Revolt (KE ~400–430, TIM-02).
- **Succession:** dies KE 430. Succeeded by Zharan I.

**Zharan I (the Famine/Epidemic King, KE 430–480)**
- **Character:** the famine/epidemic king — the great famine (the catastrophic wind-failure), the great epidemic (the "plague"); the dynasty's legitimacy is broken.
- **Key events:** the Great Famine (KE ~450–480), the Great Epidemic (KE ~460–490, TIM-02).
- **Succession:** dies KE 480. Succeeded by Maren I (the "eldest" agnate — the system fails to choose the "most worthy").

**Maren I (the Weak King, KE 480–520)**
- **Character:** the weak king — the Shreshtha's dominance (the Maratha Peshwa logic); the king is a figurehead, the Shreshtha controls the army, the revenue, the administration.
- **Key events:** the Shreshtha's Dominance (KE ~480–500, TIM-02).
- **Succession:** dies KE 520. **The Great Succession Crisis** (KE ~520–560, TIM-02) — the "eldest" (Kesra III) vs. the "most worthy" (Threna I), the temple split, the chiefs back different claimants, the Phre backs the claimant who serves their interest. The empire is divided.

### Era 5 — The Fragmentation (500–600+)

**Kesra III (the Contested King — the "eldest," the Tarn east, KE 525–590)** [concurrent]
- **Character:** the contested king — the "eldest" agnate, backed by the house's elders + the Tarn chiefs; holds the Tarn east (the fragmentation).
- **Key events:** the Great Succession Crisis (KE ~520–560, TIM-02) — the division of the empire; the Vethra's Rise (KE ~550–650, TIM-02) — the Tarn east is the southern drain.
- **Succession:** deposed/dies KE 590. The Tarn east becomes a successor state.

**Threna I (the Divided King — the "most worthy," the bār core, KE 525–650)** [concurrent]
- **Character:** the divided king — the "most worthy" younger agnate, backed by the Shreshtha + the Phre + the core chiefs; holds the bār core + the Sareth west + the Khor north (the fragmentation).
- **Key events:** the Great Succession Crisis (KE ~520–560, TIM-02) — the division of the empire; the Phre's Arrival (KE ~500–560, TIM-02) — the Phre's leverage.
- **Succession:** deposed/fragmented ~KE 650–680. Succeeded (in the bār core) by Neshan I.

**Neshan I (the Last King — the bār core, KE 650–680)**
- **Character:** the last king — Threna I's successor; the House of Kesra ends ~KE 650–700 (the dynasty is deposed/fragmented).
- **Key events:** the Successor States (KE ~600–700, TIM-02) — the House of Kesra ends.
- **Succession:** the House of Kesra ends ~KE 680. The Veshna matha survives (the religious institution, the mobile node).

## 3. The Shreshthas (the specific commanders — resolves Q-012, Q-057)

The Shreshtha is a **salaried, non-hereditary** office (DYN-01 §3) — the Shreshthas are different individuals (not a single family). The key Shreshthas:

- **Gheshar I (the Founding Shreshtha, KE 0–40)** — Kesra I's general, a fellow founding-family man (not of the royal house). He establishes the Shreshtha office (the dual structure). **Resolves Q-012:** the founding Shreshtha is a senior general of the Samur warrior band, a fellow founding-family man, not of the royal house — this keeps the office *outside* the royal house (so the dual structure is real) while making it *founding* (not a later appointment).
- **Voren I (the Dominating Shreshtha, KE ~480–520)** — the Shreshtha who dominates the weak king (Maren I, KE ~480–500) — the Maratha Peshwa logic.
- **Besra I (the Crisis Shreshtha, KE ~520–580)** — the Shreshtha who backs Threna I in the Great Succession Crisis (KE ~520–560) — the Shreshtha's dominance in the Fragmentation.

## 4. The consecration (resolves Q-013 / Q-037)

- **The ritual:** a **ritual investiture + a public oath before the Veshna temple**, performed by the **head of the Veshna matha** (the guru). The king is consecrated as the **protector of dharma** (DYN-01 §3).
- **The reversibility:** **reversible in principle** (a "de-consecration" by the matha acting with the house's consent) but **rarely used** (a last resort, because de-legitimating a king is itself a legitimacy crisis).
- **The Great Succession Crisis exception:** the matha is **split** (the sanction is withheld) — the kingmaker is divided, which is why the crisis is uncontested (the sanction cannot break the tie).

## 5. The size of the royal house (resolves Q-014)

- **The royal house (the Samur agnates):** ~**10–30 senior agnates** at any time (the founding Samur warrior band's kin, plus the absorbed royal-line marriages). Enough for a **real contest** (the "eldest vs. the most worthy" + the three-way), but not so many that the succession is unmanageable.
- **The military houses:** ~**5–10 founding-family military houses** (the top commanders, the Shreshthas, the frontier generals) — the founding elite (DYN-01 §5).

## Consequences (immediate / unintended / long-term)

- **Immediate:** the **14 named kings** (the House of Kesra, KE 0–680), the **key Shreshthas** (Gheshar I, Voren I, Besra I), the **consecration ritual** (the investiture + the oath, the reversibility), the **personal-name pool** (the place-name doubling, the -an/-ar royal names, the -esh/-ath names), the **royal house size** (~10–30 agnates).
- **Unintended:** the **named agents** (the kings, the Shreshthas) are now the canon — the later narrative (if authorized) must use these names (the name-pool rule); the **concurrent rival kings** (Kesra III + Threna I) are the Mughal 1707 pattern (the fragmentation means concurrent claimants).
- **Long-term:** the **dynastic list** is the spine of the TIM-03+ (the specific reforms/wars) and the later narrative; the **name pool** is the canon name pool (the anti-patterns' pre-flight canon check); the **consecration** is the kingmaker's real power (the sanction, the de-legitimation).
